Inside a thermocouple

From the junction to the reading

  1. Junction
  2. Insulate
  3. Terminate
  4. Measure
  1. 01 Junction

    Two dissimilar metals, one junction

    In 1821 Seebeck found that a current flows in a circuit of two dissimilar metals when their junctions are at different temperatures. The measuring junction is the one exposed to the heat.

  2. 02 Insulate

    Bedded in mineral, sealed in steel

    In a mineral insulated thermocouple the two legs are packed in mineral insulation inside a metal sheath, from 1 to 6 mm across, which can be bent and formed to suit the process.

  3. 03 Terminate

    Terminated in a colour-coded plug

    The legs end in a plug coded to the thermocouple type, green for IEC Type K, with pins of different widths so it only fits one way round. Extension cable of the same type carries the signal on.

  4. 04 Measure

    Read against a known reference

    A thermocouple measures a temperature difference. The instrument holds the reference junction at a known temperature, or compensates for it, and turns the small thermoelectric voltage into a reading.
