## Science (RePORTER abstracts, plus the company site)
- Oral dissolvable / dispersible strips (ODS): thin, stamp-sized strips of oral-disintegrating polymers in which active pharmaceutical ingredients are embedded. Placed on the tongue or inside the cheek, they adhere and dissolve, releasing medicine without liquids.
- Company claims on the site: drug loading of more than 350 mg of API on a single stamp-sized strip; dissolution adjustable from seconds to slow release over multiple hours, with targeted delivery and absorption sites; custom dosing by cutting fractional pieces because API distribution is homogeneous; work on new manufacturing routes including cold extrusion.
- HIV programme (ALD-ODS): approximately 3.4 million children under 15 live with HIV and require regular treatment; worldwide estimates for HIV-related deaths among children are as high as 110,000 annually, often caused by poor patient and caregiver adherence, rooted in low palatability of existing formulations, complexity of preparation and administration, and privacy and stigma. Oak is developing an ODS supporting the standard of care fixed-dose combination antiretroviral therapy Abacavir/Lamivudine/Dolutegravir, having demonstrated proof of concept through bioequivalence studies in Phase I. Phase II: Aim 1 optimise flexibility, dosing accuracy, storage stability; Aim 2 compare the optimal ALD-ODS candidate with TRIUMEQ-PD as reference for in vivo toxicology in rodents, pharmacokinetics in dogs and in vitro stability; Aim 3 technology transfer to an integrated large-scale manufacturing process suitable for GMP compliance. The final product would be the first pediatric formulation containing all the recommended first-line HIV drugs in a single, mono-layer mucoadhesive formulation with easily adjustable dosing. Post-Phase II the data are packaged towards an Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA).
- Latent TB programme (INH-ODS and RPT-ODS): approximately one-quarter of the world's population carries latent tuberculosis infection and an estimated 5–10% of them are at risk of developing active TB in their lifetime; US TB cases rose 5% in 2021 and a further 3.7% in 2022. Rifapentine and isoniazid are available only in large tablet forms; barriers include swallowing difficulty, unpalatable taste, weight-based dosing and side effects; rifapentine is not stable under long exposure to moisture, heat or light, so the drugs are encapsulated and captured within a support matrix. Phase II aims: evaluate three manufacturing approaches — knife-over casting as sheets, twin screw extrusion as continuous ribbons, and dose extrusion as discrete ODS — to cut waste and cost; develop encapsulated INH and RPT using optimisation and spray drying; evaluate toxicity, PK and stability in vivo and in vitro; prepare for an ANDA submission.
- Pipeline published on the site: Xerostrip (dry mouth / xerostomia, early commercialisation stage); Isoniazid-Rifapentine (TB, advanced pre-clinical studies); Abacavir-Lamivudine-Dolutegravir (HIV, advanced pre-clinical studies); Ibuprofen (pain, proof of concept); Acetaminophen (pain, proof of concept); Amoxicillin (infections, proof of concept); Cenobamate (epilepsy, proof of concept); slow-release cough relief (cough suppressant, early development); Xerorinse (anti-plaque, proof of concept).
- Intellectual property listed on the site: "Oral Delivery of Drug Actives in Laboratory Animals using Fast Dissolving Films" (US 20150366998A); "Multi-layered High Dosage Film for Oral Administration" (US 11,185,500); "Long-Acting Continuous Oral Release from Oral Dispersing Strips (ODS)…" (in preparation); trade secrets on computational formulation and continuous manufacturing.
