## Facts used
- Home and about: the mission, value proposition (physicians, patients, liability and risk), team and advisory board, the NIH grants and the DoD grant.
- Technologies: solutions for spinal fusion, hip and long bone fractures, trauma plates and infection. The sensor is a passive metal device that attaches through existing threaded holes, with a tungsten pin in the dynamic hip screw. Statistics: 300,000+ US hip fractures a year; fixation failure about 5%, over 20% in elderly patients with comorbidities; 1-year mortality 56% if readmitted within 30 days vs 19%. The disclaimer is repeated.
- Publications: patents US10667745B2 and US11013410B2, licensed to Aravis. Papers linked: Sci Rep 2017 and 2025, IEEE TBME 2021 (abstract: cadaveric tibia, precision under 2% of body weight), Adv Funct Mater 2021.
- Links page: partners CURF, Prisma Health and others; funders NSF I-Corps, SCRA, NIH. News: SCRA $50,000 acceleration grant (2020).

## Story
A fine-line section through the fractured neck and head, with the screw and pin. Fix: the pin draws in. Load: weight bearing bends the screw, the pin stays straight, and the tip gap opens. Heal: callus bridges the fracture and bending goes to zero, shown against a weeks timeline (loading seen in weeks 3–8 in the ovine study). The drawing is schematic.
