## Product: Loris® T2 Handheld OCT System (https://www.theiaimaging.com/products)
- "the world's first commercial* handheld swept source OCT system… a cart-mounted OCT engine, a long and flexible tether, and a lightweight handheld probe that accepts interchangeable lenses."
- Specifications as published: light source 1060 nm swept-source laser; image acquisition up to 500 frames/second; volume acquisition up to 4 volumes/second; axial resolution 6 microns (in tissue); imaging depth 7.3 mm (tissue) / 10.0 mm (air); ultra-mobile cart with 10 foot (3 m) tether; scan head weight < 350 g with lens.
- Three published claims: FASTER — "300kHz A-scan rate provides an order of magnitude reduction in image acquisition time"; LIGHTER — "under 350 grams (12 oz) … less than a quarter of the weight of competitive devices"; EASIER — "ergonomic design that simplifies image alignment. Infrared illumination eliminates the need for a bright, uncomfortable flash."
- Lenses: Non-contact retina lens — 20 mm working distance, 62° FOV, 10 µm lateral resolution. Ultra-widefield retina lens — 145° FOV, 24 µm lateral resolution, requires coupling fluid (artificial tears). FOVs reported using the "eye angle" definition.
- Regulatory status as published: "The T2 Handheld OCT system is under development and has not been cleared or approved by the FDA. It is available for research use only. Images shown were obtained using an investigational device in IRB-approved clinical studies."
- FDA Breakthrough Device Designation granted to the Loris T2 for retinopathy of prematurity (company post, Apr 2026): "our next-generation Handheld OCT device – the Loris T2 – has officially been granted Breakthrough Device Designation by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)." The post also states current ROP standard of care is binocular indirect ophthalmoscopy (BIO), which "relies on a manual technique that is subjective and lacks depth resolution… requires intense light and mechanical scleral depression to view the peripheral retina, a process that induces physiological stress in premature infants and can trigger bradycardia or apnea."
- Distribution: Spective is described as Theia's "dedicated distribution partner for research imaging" (ARVO 2026 post, Booth 4003).

## Funding (NIH RePORTER, org "THEIA IMAGING")
- 2R44EY034418-02A1 — "Ultrahigh Speed, Widefield OCT Angiography System for Patients who are Unable to Cooperate", NEI, award notice 2026-07-17, first-year award $1,031,041, project period 2022-09-01 to 2028-04-30, PIs Al-Hafeez Zahir Dhalla and Christian Viehland. Company release: "a two-year Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) Phase II award of approximately $2.1M from the National Eye Institute."
  - Aims (abstract): "Development of an enhanced imaging depth, 800 kHz OCTA engine"; "Development of OCTA segmentation and image capture workflow… train a neural network to provide the segmentation needed for OCTA"; "Q Submission, Pivotal Study and 510(k) Submission."
  - Context from the abstract: "Under previous NIH funding, Theia Imaging has developed the T1-A, a 600 kHz OCTA system and the T2 a commercial OCT system with a modular scanner." The new module is "the Theia angiography module (the T2-A)".
- 5R44EY033604-03 / 2R44EY033604-02 — "A Widefield, Handheld OCT system…", NEI, $979,292 (2024-08-14) and $970,759 (2025-08-13). Abstract: the Phase I T1-W was "a light weight, high speed, contact HH-OCT system with a 110° FOV… operates at 300 kHz (9x faster than any commercial handheld), its handpiece weighs 325g, and does not require mydriasis."
- 2R44EY032394-02 / 5R44EY032394-03 — "A High-Speed, User-Friendly, Handheld OCT System…", NEI, $992,089 (2023-05-26) and $938,909 (2024-07-18).
- Phase I awards: 1R43EY032394-01 ($259,612, 2021), 1R43EY033604-01A1 ($259,613, 2022), 1R43EY034418-01 ($259,603, 2022), plus supplements (2022, 2023, 2025, 2026).
- Clinical context from award abstracts: OCT is "the gold standard for diagnosis of retinal diseases"; OCT angiography "enables non-invasive, depth resolved imaging of retinal microvasculature without the need for exogenous contrast agents"; infants at risk for retinopathy of prematurity "make up 1.4% of the newborn population."
