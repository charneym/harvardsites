## Science (abstract, verbatim excerpts)
- Late-stage AF: "Intermediate and late-stage AF are more CA-resistant, with recurrence of AF being related to more complex target substrate primarily localized to the posterior wall of the left atrium."
- "epicardial adipose tissue (EAT) on the outer surface of the left atrium, is common in later stage AF and is known to be associated with CA failures."
- Need: "a device that can durably ablate contiguous EAT and atrial muscle without bipolar clamping from the outer surface of the left atrium on a warm beating heart."
- "the patented HeartPad Cryoablation System, powered by liquid helium (-269°C) and high-efficiency heat exchangers…full-thickness ablation of heart tissue from the outside of a warm and full beating heart despite EAT coverage and without the need to open a heart chamber or use cardiopulmonary bypass."
- Aim 1: refine prototype for posterior wall epicardial ablation. Aim 2: subsurface tissue temperatures during mock ablations of validated synthetic human left atrium tissue blocks with progressively thicker EAT in a heart simulator. Phase II: design freeze and regulatory testing in preparation for FDA submission.
- Narrative: "Late-stage Atrial Fibrillation (AF) represents up to 40% or more of the entire AF population but comprises only ~10% of all ablation procedures"; "substantially more cooling power than commercial devices and preliminary acute animal and cadaver work has been published".

## Device (https://www.corfigo.com/products/ and home page)
- Home: "intended for ablation of cardiac tissue from an epicardial (outer surface of the heart) location, while simultaneously protecting adjacent structures…Designed for open, MIS surgical and percutaneous ablation…liquid helium…transmural ablation of diseased cardiac tissue despite barriers of thick surface fat and warm cardiac blood flow. Cryoablation may be associated with better preservation of extracellular architecture and less inflammation compared with other thermal energy sources."
- Products: positioned "using direct vision, video guidance or in conjunction with cardiac mapping"; expanded "by inflating its pressure-controlled insulation pontoon"; "Extremely low temperature ('XLT') helium gas (colder than -250C) is driven through the unique ablating surface, attaching the ablation pad to the heart by a process called cryo-adhesion"; adjacent areas "protected from ablation injury by the insulation pontoon"; "warm helium gas is supplied to thaw the ablation pad which reverses the cryo-adhesion and allows re-positioning"; then withdrawn from the pericardial cavity.
- Regulatory wording: the products page says "The HeartPad® is not for sale inside or outside the United States." while every footer says "NOT FOR SALE IN THE UNITED STATES". Because the two contradict each other, the concept site states no sale status.
- Development pipeline (products page image): "HeartPad® Ribbon (adjustable length): 'box lesion'" and "HeartPad® Ribbon (adjustable length): mitral isthmus, VT, other".
- Videos (Vimeo, CorFigo account): "HeartPadJul2021" https://vimeo.com/580033573 (2:25); "HPMini 2022 VT" https://vimeo.com/703714573 (2:55).

## Publications (PubMed)
- Liuba I, Younis A, Sperling J, … Santangeli P. "Efficacy of balloon-expandable extreme-low-temperature ventricular epicardial cryoablation: A preclinical proof of concept evaluation." Heart Rhythm 2025;22(7):e183-e191. PMID 39306265. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39306265/
  Ten swine, surgical (n=6) and subxiphoid percutaneous (n=4) access; 42 epicardial lesions; console vaporizes liquid helium (-269°C); ablation element on an expandable insulation pontoon. Mean depth, surgical: 11±2 mm ≤30 s, 13±4 mm 60 s, 15±3 mm ≥120 s; percutaneous: 10±2, 14±2, 16±2 mm at 30/60/120 s. "Lesion geometry seemed unaffected by presence and thickness of epicardial fat." One episode of VF after ablation over the AV groove and 2 adjacent obtuse marginal arteries. Conclusion: feasible, "can efficiently produce deep ventricular lesions in different epicardial locations." Cleveland Clinic authors; Santangeli received a research grant from Corfigo.
- Sperling JS, Santangeli P. "Ablation options for sub-epicardially located ventricular substrates responsible for ventricular tachycardia: where is it all headed?" Curr Opin Cardiol 2025;40(1):1-7. PMID 39387703.
- Sperling JS, Delurgio DL, Svinarich JT. "Posterior left atrial epicardial adipose tissue: scope of the problem and impact of new technology." Curr Opin Cardiol 2022;37(1):54-61. PMID 34508033.
