One sealed chamber, flexing.

How a diaphragm pump works, the principle Charles Austen has refined since 1919.

IntakeExhaustSealed

Drawn in.

An eccentric on the motor shaft pulls the connecting rod down. The diaphragm flexes away from the head, the inlet valve opens and air or gas is drawn into the chamber.

Pushed out.

On the up-stroke the diaphragm flexes back, the inlet closes and the outlet valve opens: vacuum on one port, pressure on the other, from the same unit.

Sealed throughout.

The chamber is totally sealed, with no sliding seal between moving parts, so pumping stays contamination free, on continuous duty with long life and low maintenance.

Six principles, invented in house.

Charles Austen Pumps invents all of its own pump technology and invests in R&D to design, develop and prove each new product, so our engineers know every element of the pumps we make.

Rotary diaphragmPatented
An eccentric bearing on the drive shaft revolves inside a flexible diaphragm, squeezing liquid through the pump. No valves, non-clogging.
Diaphragm
The pumping chamber is totally sealed, with no sliding seal between moving parts; vacuum and pressure come from the flexing diaphragm.
Linear diaphragm
A magnetically operated diaphragm flexes at one or both ends of the connecting rod, keeping wear and power consumption to a minimum.
Rocking piston
The higher performance of a piston pump with the simplicity of a diaphragm pump, in a small, economic unit built for pressure.
GyRok™Patented
A conical elastomer diaphragm displaced by legs around a nutating drive: whisper quiet, self-priming, tolerant of dry running, any orientation.
Vibrating armature
A minimum-component design that is exceptionally economical and quiet, made for household fish tanks and other everyday duty.

A century of pumps where it mattered.

From the laboratory to Everest, the Apollo programme and the first IVF birth, Charles Austen pumps have been specified for work that could not fail. A few moments from our history.

  1. 1919

    The compact diaphragm pump

    Charles Austen is widely believed to have built the first compact diaphragm pump, with left-over army boot leather as the diaphragm.

  2. 1950s

    Pumps in the laboratory

    Charles Austen Pumps became the first manufacturer to put pumps in the laboratory.

  3. 1953

    On Everest

    Hillary and Tenzing took a Charles Austen pump to the summit for high-altitude experiments.

  4. 1954

    Byfleet, and one family

    Renamed Charles Austen Pumps Ltd and moved to Byfleet, under the same family ownership to this day.

  5. 1971

    Apollo 15

    Our pumps created a vacuum chamber, like the atmosphere of space, to test components of the lunar rover.

  6. 1984

    The first IVF pump

    The first manufacturer of a pump for the IVF conception process, used in the birth of Louise Brown.

  7. 1994

    The Channel Tunnel

    Specified for the air monitoring system underground between England and France.

  8. 2009

    Portable medical suction

    Designed and built the pump for a portable suction device now standard in most US emergency response vehicles.
