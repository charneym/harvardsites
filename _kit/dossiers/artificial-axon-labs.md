# Artificial Axon Labs Inc. — dossier (facts with sources)

## Company
- MIT spinout developing Artificial Axons, a biomimetic 3D-printed drug-discovery platform for remyelinating therapies, initial focus multiple sclerosis. — RePORTER abstract https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11409835 ; https://www.artificialaxonlabs.com/
- Vision: "Transforming the discovery of medicines for neurodegeneration for every patient by bringing together engineered biomaterials, 3D printing, and human cells." — https://www.artificialaxonlabs.com/
- Science: direct measurement of myelination to discover first-in-class remyelinating drugs; combines 3D printing of axon mimics and patient-derived human cells. Light-based 3D printing of sub-micrometer features in ultra-low-stiffness biocompatible materials; only assay with direct 3D quantification of myelination in an axon-like environment; compatible with human cells. — https://www.artificialaxonlabs.com/
- Contact: anna.jagielska@artificialaxonlabs.com; LinkedIn https://linkedin.com/company/artificial-axon-labs/ ; X https://twitter.com/ArtificialAxons. RePORTER location: Topsfield, MA 01983. Time zone: US Eastern.
- Platform validated at MIT with industry and academic collaborators (site lists Sanofi, NYSCF, Harvard, MGH, MIT logos).

## Funding (NIH RePORTER)
- 2R44NS135867-02, NINDS, SBIR Phase II, notice 2026-08-06, first-year $1,478,216, PI Anna Jagielska; 2026-08-06 to 2028-07-31. "In vitro myelination assay modeling the environment of MS lesions for predictive discovery of remyelinating therapies." Milestones: (1) myelination assay including inflammatory factors that inhibit myelin repair in MS lesions (interferon gamma and myelin debris); (2) validate by testing how efficacy of known pro-myelinating compounds changes in the lesion-like environment. Axon mimics match geometry and low stiffness of biological axons; existing 3D assays (glass cones, electrospun fibres) are orders of magnitude stiffer than nervous tissue. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11409835
- 1R43NS135867-01, NINDS, Phase I, 2024-09-12, $506,083. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/10822538
