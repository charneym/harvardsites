## Product / science (only as stated)
- SONOflammation: "a noninvasive, home-use system that integrates biomarker monitoring with an ultrasound stimulator to reduce inflammation and dependence on immunosuppressant drugs in kidney transplant patients. This closed-loop system aims to detect and treat transplant rejection in real-time." Innovate@BU team profile: https://www.bu.edu/innovate/profile/inia-biosciences
- MTEC listing: development-stage device that "combines non-invasive ultrasound stimulation with integrated diagnostic sensing and closed-loop control for home-administered management of chronic inflammatory diseases"; expertise: bioelectronic medicine, ultrasound neuromodulation.
- NSF STTR Phase I abstract: "The external system stimulates targeted nerves in the spleen to modulate the immune system through established physiologic pathways." Objectives: (1) a preclinical transducer delivering the therapeutic ultrasonic waveform to the targeted splenic nerves; (2) optimize treatment parameters in an accepted preclinical skin allograft model; (3) validate reduction in pro-inflammatory cytokines in situ. Context: over 100,000 kidney transplants worldwide each year, up to 20% of patients experience rejection; immunosuppressants have significant side effects and cost nearly $30,000 per patient per year; extensible to other organ transplants and chronic inflammatory diseases.
- NSF Project Outcomes Report (last modified 2025-07-16, by Dragana Savic): precise ultrasound aimed at a specific immune organ, without surgery, as a drug-free way to alter the immune state; via "the natural nerve-based process that control[s] inflammation"; transplant mouse model. Phase I achievements: safe ultrasound settings that reduce inflammation without heat or tissue damage, safe for repeated use; treatment timing tuned from blood samples to consistently reach the spleen at the optimal time; "Blood biomarker analysis revealed a statistically significant reduction in key inflammation-related markers following ultrasound treatment, and repeated treatments led to a meaningful delay in transplant rejection"; a custom modular hardware system for spleen-targeted ultrasound in small animals; protocols, timing schedules and biomarker assays. Next: integrate into patient care; scalable platform for other therapeutic areas.
- BU Today / The Brink, 13 Oct 2021 (https://www.bu.edu/articles/2021/bu-entrepreneurs-move-two-new-medical-devices-closer-to-market/): "noninvasive, wearable medical device that uses ultrasound technology to reduce the likelihood of a patient’s body rejecting a newly transplanted organ"; works "by suppressing this immune response by modulating certain nerve signals"; could "track the progress of organ acceptance into the body, sending an alert if rejection begins early on". Team then: Shen Ning, Dragana Savic, Pablo Elvira. Ning: transplant patients are put on immunosuppressants "on a trial and error basis", costing more than $30,000 every year per patient.
