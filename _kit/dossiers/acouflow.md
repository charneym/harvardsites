## Activity (pre-screen: OK)
- **NIH NHLBI STTR Phase I, 1R41HL182396-01** — "Toward Establishing the Feasibility and Commercial Readiness of VortexPAP for
  Improving OSA Treatment". Org: ACOUFLOW THERAPEUTICS, LLC, Cincinnati, OH 45244. Start 2026-08-10, end 2027-07-31; $497,857.
  Contact PI Liran Oren; PI Ann Romaker. https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11253024
  Abstract (used for hero, story, programme): VortexPAP, a novel PAP therapy for mild to moderate OSA without a sealed mask;
  vortical airflow generates therapeutic pressures without a tight seal; aims: (1) Phase 0 feasibility clinical trial of safety and
  effectiveness; (2) optimise for home use with compliance monitoring, usable without clinical supervision; (3) trial protocol,
  informed consent documents and operational manuals for larger multi-center trials; next phase compares long-term adherence and
  efficacy with conventional CPAP; testing performed in collaboration with the University of Cincinnati.
- Not pathogens/toxins/biodefence: a respiratory therapy device.

## Background coverage (University of Cincinnati)
- UC News, 2024-04-17, "UC researchers develop new CPAP device" (Cedric Ricks):
  https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/2024/04/university-of-cincinnati-researchers-develop-a-new-cpap-device-to-assist-indivials-with-obstructive-sleep-apnea.html
  ~40 million US adults with OSA; noncompliance estimated to affect at least a third of patients; vortex airflow, a flow-control
  concept from aerospace engineering; minimalist mask that barely touches the face; lower pressures reduce air swallowing;
  not tight or enclosed, should help people with claustrophobia (Romaker); preliminary clinical study with current CPAP users:
  delivered their therapy pressure levels, mask more comfortable; UC Venture Lab support; team: Liran Oren PhD (research associate
  professor, Otolaryngology–HNS, UC College of Medicine; leads the work with engineers), Ephraim Gutmark PhD (aerospace
  engineering), Ann Romaker MD (director, UC Sleep Medicine Center; professor of internal medicine; identified main reasons for
  CPAP noncompliance), Roy Kulick MD (UC entrepreneur-in-residence, business plan).
- WVXU via UC, 2024-04-22: "each pulse is a vortex, similar to a smoke ring"; quote (Oren): "The vortex pulses can pressurize the
  airway in the same manner as CPAP devices, we just don't need the seal anymore."
  https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/2024/04/wvxu--find-your-cpap-annoying-university-of-cincinnati-researchers-may-have-a-solution.html
- NIH SEED / NCAI proof-of-concept project (UC; completed): pulsed vortex airflow; develop and verify an ergonomic design.
  https://seed.nih.gov/portfolio/nih-funded-projects/poc-funded-tech/application-vortex-airflow-continuous-positive-airway
