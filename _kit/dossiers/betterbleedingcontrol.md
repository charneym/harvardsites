## Product / science
- NIH abstract: uncontrolled hemorrhage remains the leading cause of preventable death in military combat; extremity and junctional injuries nearly 50% of such fatalities; low skill retention; current models lack objective feedback and need significant instructor resources; trauma costs exceed $660B/yr in the US. Aim 1: calibrate pressure sensors (±5% of applied force, ≥85% accuracy of integrated sensors); self-contained fluid reservoir in the torso (sessions ≥60 min). Aim 2: sensor placement ≥90% force detection coverage, accuracy ±15%; visual/auditory biofeedback, flow-rate change recognised within 0.5 s. Immediate: military; long-term: peri-military and civilian (responders, medical institutions, public safety agencies).
- Capstone: pressure sensors with tactile, audio and visual feedback; simulates active bleeding; responds to tourniquet or direct pressure; generates performance reports; two wounds each with its own solenoid; single reservoir to a pump (stepper motor, Raspberry Pi).
