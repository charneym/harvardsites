## Funding (NIH RePORTER, org "POTOMAC AFFINITY PROTEINS")
- **2R44GM140633-02A1 — "Selection system for identifying protein-specific folding tags that enable purification of native cytokines from E. coli", NIGMS, award notice 2026-06-09, first-year award $1,109,087, project period through 2028-05-31, PI Philip N Bryan.**
  - Abstract: "Our overall objective is to develop and test tags that enable folding of growth factors in E. coli and facilitate purification of the tag-free, native protein. Numerous growth factors are needed for cell culture, but most are difficult to express and costly to purify… In Phase I we developed a method to identify dual-function tags that guide the formation of native disulfide bonds in E. coli and then bind tightly to the processing protease and direct tag removal in the purification phase."
  - Phase II plan: design tags for the remaining growth factors most used in cell culture; "develop a scalable process for producing pre-GMP quality growth factors and validate physical properties and biological activity. Gram qualities of validated TGFb1, VEGFA, and IGF1 will be produced in Phase II."
  - Narrative: "Folding tags facilitate purification of the key growth factors needed to create defined, synthetic cell culture media and aid in the reliable and economical production of therapeutic cells."
- **1R43GM159468-01A1 — "Self-amplifying protease chain reactions for simple, sensitive, and quantitative detection", NIGMS, award notice 2026-06-23, $377,602, project period 2026-07-01 to 2027-06-30, PI Philip N Bryan.** Abstract describes three components — "a protease conjugated to a binding molecule", "an inhibitor protein which contains a proteolytic cleavage site for the protease it inhibits", and "a protease substrate which generates a signal upon its cleavage" — and the Phase I targets: "gram-negative bacterial lipopolysaccharide (endotoxin), human interleukin-2 (IL2), and human ferritin", benchmarked against the Limulus Amebocyte Lysate assay and sandwich ELISA.
- Earlier NIH awards to the company (selected): 1R43GM140633-01A1 ($224,806, 2021); R44GM126676 affinity-tag purification Phase II ($760,681 in 2019, $729,996 in 2020); R44CA163403 protease chain reactions for cancer markers (2011–2016); R44RR025973 / R44GM103389 engineered proteases for proteomics (2009–2014); 1R43GM076786-01 proteases and tags for protein purification (2006), the company's first award.
- Mechanism, from the R44GM126676 abstract: "The transformative purification technology combines three components: 1) a regulated, highly-specific protease (Psub) that is constitutively inactive; 2) a small molecule (imidazole) that activates the Psub; 3) a high-affinity inhibitor (Pro). Immobilized Psub (in the off-state) can capture a Pro-fusion protein from a cellular extract, allowing contaminants to be washed away. Subsequent addition of imidazole to the immobilized complex releases pure, tag-free target protein and leaves the Pro-tag tightly bound to Psub."

## Pro-CR (the company's detection technology; https://www.potomac-affinity-proteins.com/pro-cr and steps 1–3)
- "Pro-CR combines a series of enzymatic components to create amplifying chain reactions. The goals of Pro-CR are: 1) to detect vanishingly small quantities of an initiator molecule by exponentially amplifying its signal and 2) to determine the original concentration of the initiator molecule by using the time signature of the reaction."
- Step 1 — "Detection of an initiator protease is key to the Pro-CR Technology… The red initiator cuts the cleavage sequence of the inhibitor, activating the blue protease." Example given: "In the ProCR version of an ELISA… the target protein is immobilized on a solid support, an antibody conjugated to an initiating protease forms a complex with the target protein, and then self-amplifying complex and substrate are added to amplify the signal from the conjugated protease and convert its concentration into a time signature."
- Step 2 — "Because the blue protease can cut the inhibitor of the blue complex, it sets off a chain reaction that amplifies the original signal exponentially."
- Step 3 — "The key to detection is the addition of a signaling peptide. Intact, it does not provide any fluorescent signal. However, it can be cleaved by free protease which activates the fluorescence."
- 2026 Phase I abstract adds the self-amplification mechanism: "Self-amplification is triggered when polyclonal IgG forms an immune complex around the target. The immune complexes concentrate proteases resulting in a critical state and rapid self-activation of all bound proteases… analogous to the amplification of DNA by a polymerase chain reaction."
