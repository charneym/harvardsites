RVP-001·Manganese-based·Phase 2·FDA Fast Track

Gadolinium-freecontrast-enhancedMRI.

RVP-001 is a manganese-based MRI contrast agent designed as a direct replacement for today’s standard-of-care agents. It is in Phase 2 clinical studies, with FDA Fast Track designation.

01Contrast

Contrast makes the invisible visible.

A contrast agent does not glow. It shortens the time nearby water protons take to relax back along the scanner’s magnetic field, so tissue where it gathers returns a stronger signal and appears bright on a T1-weighted image.

40M+ contrast-enhanced MRI scans each year · Reveal

02The gadolinium question

Today’s agents are built on gadolinium.

The FDA states that gadolinium can remain in the body, including the brain, for months to years after an MRI, and that linear agents retain more than macrocyclic ones. Retention has not been directly linked to harmful effects in patients with normal kidney function.

Since 2018 every gadolinium-based agent carries an FDA class warning and patient Medication Guide · FDA Drug Safety Communication, 2017 / 2018

03Manganese

Hold the metal. Let the water move.

Manganese is a biocompatible element essential for life and, like gadolinium, strongly paramagnetic. The chelator’s six donor atoms hold the ion tightly; one site stays open. A water molecule binds there, its protons relax, and it swaps out for the next, many millions of times a second.

Schematic: six prongs for six donor atoms, one open seat for water

04The image

Where it gathers, the image brightens.

Relaxation spreads through the surrounding water and the tissue lights up. In the founders’ animal studies, the manganese complex Mn-PyC3A gave vessel and tumor enhancement comparable to gadolinium agents.

Procedural axial slice, not a patient image · Radiology 2018 · Investigative Radiology 2019

Why RVP-001

All current MRI contrast agents contain gadolinium. RVP-001 is a direct replacement, based on manganese.

Reveal’s position: gadolinium is a rare earth element that accumulates in patients’ brains and bodies, adds to gadolinium pollution in surface waters, and relies on a supply chain concentrated in China. Until now there has been no viable replacement.

Phase 2 · NCT06322342

Enhancement, measured against gadolinium.

A Phase 2 ascending-dose safety and efficacy study of RVP-001 in adults with known gadolinium-enhancing brain or spinal lesions, such as brain tumors or multiple sclerosis lesions.

Each participant serves as their own comparison: the same lesions are imaged with gadolinium and with RVP-001.

Ages 18–75, acceptable renal function, at least one enhancing lesion ≥ 5 mm.

  1. Gadolinium MRI

    A standard gadolinium-enhanced MRI, 2 to 14 days before dosing.

  2. Single dose of RVP-001

    One of three ascending dose levels, followed by contrast-enhanced MRI so the two images can be compared directly.

  3. Follow-up MRI

    An unenhanced MRI 1 to 6 weeks later.

Published science

Invented at MGH. Peer-reviewed at every step.

RVP-001 was invented by Peter Caravan and Eric Gale at Harvard Medical School and Massachusetts General Hospital. Their published work on the manganese complex Mn-PyC3A is the scientific foundation of the program; MGH’s 2017 release noted Reveal’s option to license Mn-PyC3A, and the NIDDK grant abstract names RVP-001 as the lead manganese chelate.

  • J. Am. Chem. Soc. · 2015

    A manganese alternative to gadolinium for MRI contrast

    Relaxivity in blood plasma comparable to commercial gadolinium agents; among the most stable Mn²⁺ complexes at pH 7.4; about 20-fold more resistant to dissociation than Gd-DTPA under a zinc challenge; renal and hepatobiliary clearance, over 99% eliminated by 24 h.

    PMC4764508 ↗
  • Radiology · online Nov 2017 · 2018;286:865

    Gadolinium-free MR angiography in baboons

    Mn-PyC3A enhanced blood vessels comparably to Gd-DTPA. Dynamic imaging showed rapid blood clearance and elimination through both kidneys and the hepatobiliary system, with no evidence of free manganese release.

    doi 10.1148/radiol.2017170977 ↗
  • Investigative Radiology · 2019

    Tumor enhancement and whole-body elimination

    Erstad et al.: in mouse models of breast cancer and metastatic liver cancer, tumor enhancement was comparable to Gd-DOTA and Gd-EOB-DTPA; in rats the agent was eliminated more completely than either gadolinium agent.

    doi 10.1097/RLI.0000000000000593 ↗
  • Investigative Radiology · 2020

    PET-MRI pharmacokinetics and biodistribution

    Zhou et al.: cleared mainly by the kidneys, with a smaller fraction via the liver; in renally impaired rats the liver compensated, and whole-body manganese cleared more efficiently at 7 days than gadolinium after gadoterate.

    doi 10.1097/RLI.0000000000000736 ↗

“Without a chelator of sufficient strength, the manganese will be taken up by the liver and remain in the body.”

Peter Caravan, PhD

Animal and in-vitro findings are the founders’ published results, not clinical results for RVP-001.
