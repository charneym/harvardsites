# OncoSTING, LLC — factual dossier
Sources: oncosting.com (crawled 2026-11-12), NIH RePORTER API.

## Company
- OncoSTING LLC, Baltimore, Maryland (NIH RePORTER organisation record). Site footer: "info@OncoSTING.com · © 2026 OncoSTING LLC". Time zone: US Eastern.
- Positioning (home): "OncoSTING is developing novel bladder cancer therapies that work in two ways — Harnessing the potency of standard BCG immunotherapy; Long-lived delivery of potent STING agonists using BCG as the delivery vehicle."
- Technology page: "OncoSTING is developing recombinant STING agonist immunotherapies including OS-151 and others. These live BCG-based immunotherapies are engineered to deliver sustained release of potent STING agonists and to retain the known efficacy of traditional BCG against bladder cancer."
- "OS-151 is a second-generation BCG immunotherapy for bladder cancer which produces high levels of a potent STING agonist and at the same time retains the known efficacy of traditional BCG. OncoSTING's preclinical research and development has revealed that OS-151 is more effective than traditional BCG in multiple models of bladder cancer. Additionally, OS-151 has an improved safety profile compared to traditional BCG in preclinical studies."
- Indications named on the site: **Bladder cancer** — "Bladder cancer afflicts ~80,000 US individuals annually. Two-thirds of cases are non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). NMIBC patients receive intravesical traditional BCG immunotherapy but 30% will have BCG unresponsive disease." **Solid tumors** — "Tumors such as malignant melanoma have been treated successfully with STING agonists and also traditional BCG. OS-151 is being developed as an intratumoral immunotherapy for certain solid tumors."

## Funding (NIH RePORTER, org "ONCOSTING")
- **1R44CA310037-01 — "STING agonist-expressing BCG to enhance bladder cancer therapy", NCI, award notice 2026-09-10, first-year award $1,030,215, project period 2026-09-10 to 2028-08-31, PIs William Ramses Bishai and Todd Wallach. A Direct to Phase II SBIR.**
  - Abstract: "OncoSTING's novel rBCG-STING overexpresses c-di-AMP, a potent agonist of STING (STimulator of INterferon Genes). STING agonists potentiate antitumor responses through the STING-IRF3-NF-kB immune pathway. The BCG-STING therapeutic exploits BCG's prolonged intracellular survival to provide sustained STING agonist delivery from the intracellular space and provides a two-pronged mechanism of action: BCG-mediated trained immunity, as well as STING agonist-mediated induction of interferon-I and other proinflammatory mediators."
  - Problem statement: "Bladder cancer is the fourth most common cancer in men and approximately 75% of new patients present with non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). The first line of treatment for NMIBC is intravesical BCG, but patients suffer from high relapse rates, 30% of patients do not respond, there is a 4% rate of systemic bacteremia, and there are manufacturing shortages."
  - Four aims: dose-response studies to optimise dose and regimen (ISG reporter macrophages, MB49 and BBN mouse models); a non-GLP dose-range finding study in rabbits; "Fermentation-based manufacturing of a GMP batch. BCG-STING has already been transferred to ArrantaBio-ReciBioPharm, a CDMO with experience manufacturing BCG"; and a GLP toxicology study, as IND-enabling work.
  - Narrative: "OncoSTING's Direct to Phase II project will accelerate the submission of an IND for BCG-STING, allowing for the first-in-human trials in patients with recurrent non-muscle invasive bladder cancer, particularly in Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG)-experienced patients."
- **TB vaccine programme (NIAID):** 1R44AI181290-01A1 ($270,176, 2024-06-20), 4R44AI181290-02 ($991,112, 2025-08-25), 5R44AI181290-03 ($986,921, 2026-07-22) — "BCG overexpressing a small molecule STING agonist as a vaccine for TB", PI Todd Wallach. Abstract: "OncoSTING is developing OS-101 and OS-151, recombinant versions of BCG engineered to overexpress the potent STING agonist c-di-AMP for a two-pronged mechanism of action… OncoSTING is the only company developing STING agonist delivery by a live bacterial vaccine—BCG." It also explains the OS-101 → OS-151 step: "the FDA does not allow advanced human clinical trials with BCG strains harboring antibiotic resistance. OncoSTING has therefore generated OS-151, an antibiotic resistance-free version of OS-101."
- **Earlier bladder cancer awards (NCI):** 1R44CA261506-01A1 ($244,436, 2021-08-31, PI Shaaretha Pelly), 4R44CA261506-02 ($907,018, 2022-07-19), 5R44CA261506-03 ($1,092,982, 2023-08-28), PI Todd Wallach. The 2021 abstract records the preclinical comparison: "Compared with wild type BCG, OS-101 demonstrates superior antitumor efficacy in models of NMIBC; more potent pro-inflammatory cytokine responses; greater myeloid cell reprogramming, producing an M1 shift with enhanced phagocytosis/autophagy; more pronounced epigenetic changes in key cytokine promoter regions; and metabolomic changes favoring antitumor immunity."
- Total NIH support across the two programmes, by award notice amounts above: $5.52M across seven project years (2021–2026).
