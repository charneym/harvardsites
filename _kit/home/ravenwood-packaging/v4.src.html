The Circle of Linerless

From the coater to the shelf

  1. Coat
  2. Apply
  3. Inspect
  4. Shelf
  1. 01 Coat

    Adhesive below, release strips on top

    The Com 500 applies pressure-sensitive adhesive on the back and silicone release strips on the face in a controlled pattern, so the labels self-wind without a liner.

  2. 02 Apply

    Cut and applied on the line

    Printed by our approved printers, the reels run on Nobac applicators. Each label is cut and applied straight onto the pack, and only the adhesive touches the product.

  3. 03 Inspect

    Every pack checked

    Vision checks label position, print, barcodes and use-by dates. X-ray finds glass, metal, ceramics and bone, even through printed film and aluminium trays.

  4. 04 Shelf

    No liner left behind

    No backing paper goes to landfill, and even the edge trimmings are recyclable. Our linerless label technology can be found along all major supermarket aisles.

Labels & Materials

Why linerless?

Linerless labels are pressure-sensitive labels supplied without a backing liner. Silicone release strips and adhesive zones let them self-wind and apply cleanly on Nobac applicators, in paper and polypropylene, with adhesives optimised for each product.

  • Zero waste liner to landfillEven the waste edge trimmings are recyclable.
  • Slash carbon emissionsReduced transport, labour and storage costs.
  • More surface to printFeature additional information on the back of the label.

5 billionRavenwood linerless labels produced worldwide in 2025

That is equivalent to 201,000 miles, enough to circle the Earth over eight times. The resulting CO2 savings amount to 12,500 tonnes.

About Ravenwood

Leaders in linerless since 2004

We are leaders in linerless label technology and experts in pack inspection. We build, manufacture and supply Nobac linerless applicators, linerless converting equipment and pack inspection systems, and supply coding and conventional self-adhesive labelling equipment.

The Nobac range is designed and built in our Norwich factory. Over 90% of a machine's weight is produced in the UK, with parts made on site or sourced within a four-mile radius of the factory.
