How it works · Interpretive visualization
01 The overactive pathway

A pathway thatwill not rest.

In disorders such as trigeminal neuropathic pain, a defined neural pathway fires relentlessly, causing sudden, intense pain that is often drug-resistant. Today's treatments act broadly, on whole networks.

02 eOPN3

A light sensor, placedonly in chosen cells.

Gene therapy delivers eOPN3, a proprietary, highly sensitive and stable inhibitory GPCR opsin from Prof. Ofer Yizhar's lab at the Weizmann Institute of Science, into targeted neurons. Seven helices span the membrane.

03 Light arrives

Light switchesthe sensor on.

Activated by light, eOPN3 engages the cell's own Gi/o signalling at the presynaptic terminal and inhibits neurotransmitter release. The signal is stopped at the synapse.

04 The pathway quiets

Silence the pathway.Spare the rest.

Only neurons that carry the opsin respond, and only when light is delivered. Neighbouring cells keep working normally. Precision in space and time.

The human brain holds more than 86 billionneurons. Most treatments reach whole networks.Modulight is built to reach one pathway.

Drugs, surgery and electrical stimulation act broadly, affecting large regions of the brain, with limited benefit and unwanted side effects. Optogenetics enables precise control of brain activity using light.

Optogenetics research featured in Nature, Science and Cell.
The platform · eOPN3

A new generation of optogenetics,built for medicine.

eOPN3 is a proprietary, highly sensitive and stable inhibitory GPCR opsin developed in the laboratory of Prof. Ofer Yizhar at the Weizmann Institute of Science and licensed from YEDA. Modulight describes it as the first opsin that can be used to modulate pathological activity and treat neurological disorders in humans. The approach is disease-mechanism-agnostic: it applies wherever excess activity in a defined pathway drives symptoms.

Delivery

Where it matters most

For trigeminal pain, eOPN3 can be delivered directly into the trigeminal ganglion in a simple outpatient procedure, then activated by light on demand.

Light that reaches

eOPN3 is red-shifted, and red light penetrates tissue well, so trigeminal pain conditions allow for minimally invasive light delivery.

Mechanism after Mahn M. et al., Neuron, 2021, optogenetic silencing of neurotransmitter release with a mosquito rhodopsin. Delivery: PR Newswire and Pharmaceutical-Technology, 12 Nov 2025.
