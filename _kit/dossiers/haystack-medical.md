## Science (abstract facts used)
- Inner ear encased in dense bone, protected by the blood-labyrinth barrier; systemic drugs fail to reach therapeutic levels; no clinically accepted methods to directly sample cochlear fluid or deliver drugs into the inner ear.
- Hearing loss and balance disorders will affect more than 2.5 billion people globally by 2050.
- Demonstrated in animal models and human temporal bones that ultrasharp, hair-thin microneedles enable aspiration and injection without anatomic or audiologic harm.
- Phase I: feasibility of controlled RWM perforation and aspiration in cadaveric temporal bones; access "only going through the tympanic membrane, making a hole as small as an ear tube"; draw 1 µL of fluid, enough for diagnostics. Phase I plan: 510(k) clearance of microneedle and actuation device, PMA if FDA considers it significantly different from predicates.
- Phase II: first-in-human clinical trial. Aim 1 safety of perilymph aspiration in cochlear implant patients (hearing preservation, biomarker yield, procedural feasibility). Aim 2 safety of pressure-neutral, dual-lumen microneedle drug delivery in a parallel cohort with DEX-P as model therapeutic. Expected: ≥90% procedural success with no significant hearing loss. Initially surgical settings, expanding to outpatient and office-based use; partnerships with hospitals, otolaryngology clinics, pharmaceutical companies.
