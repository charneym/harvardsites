## Science (RePORTER abstracts)
- PKAN: rare progressive neurodegenerative disorder with brain iron accumulation; early immobility, often death by early adulthood; caused by mutations in PANK2 (mitochondrial pantothenate kinase), the first enzyme in CoA synthesis from vitamin B5. No specific/established therapy.
- Symptoms include retinopathy, dystonia, spasticity, parkinsonism.
- Hypothesis: activating human PANK3 stimulates CoA production in PANK2-mutated cells.
- Chemical screen + medicinal chemistry → 14 human PANK3 activators (VTAC1-14), AC50 in nM range; no effect on PANK1/PANK2; no toxicity against four human cell lines and one primary human cell; desirable solubility.
- CoA increased in wild-type and pank2-/- human cell lines, correlating with hPANK3 activation.
- Mouse PK (VTAC-1, VTAC-2): plasma and brain exposure, excellent half-life, no apparent toxicity. Initial in vivo efficacy in whole-body knockout PKAN mouse: increased CoA with VTAC-2.
- Phase II aims: (1) characterise backup VTACs (biochemical, cell-based, PK); (2) in vivo efficacy of late lead VTAC-2 and backups in whole-body pank2-/- and SynCre pank1fl/fl pank2fl/fl mouse models. Goal: preclinical safety/efficacy toward clinical development.
- Phase I abstract: discovered PKAN has hallmarks of a mitochondrial disorder with accumulation of mitophagosomes → novel imaging biomarker distinguishing PKAN and normal cells.
- Pitch deck: screening cascade 156,593 compounds → 268 hits → 12 chemotypes → 415 analogs; IP Yale 63/043,534.
