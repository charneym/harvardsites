## Science (verbatim-sourced)
From the NHLBI Phase II abstract (1R44HL184761):
- "Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is the leading common cause of maternal mortality worldwide… over 287,000 women
  die from complications of pregnancy and childbirth each year, with mortality rates in low-income countries
  >35x that of first world countries."
- "Existing technologies are limited to mechanical tamponade and fail to address the underlying hemostatic
  dysfunction essential for effective PPH management."
- "HemoMax-PPH, a biodegradable hydrogel adhesive that combines strong adhesion energies 100 to 1,000 times
  greater on wet surfaces than existing sealants with a balloon tamponade and negative pressure system."
- "This innovative material forms a durable seal within minutes, irrespective of blood exposure, and accommodates
  in vivo dynamics."
- The R44 performs (1) bench performance of the final packaged and sterilized product, (2) ISO 10993 advanced
  biocompatibility testing, (3) a large animal GLP acute and chronic trial, toward a 510(k) for HemoMax-PPH as a
  class II device.
From the Phase I abstract (1R43HL176353): in a study of 1.6 million surgical procedures, over 30% of patients had
bleeding-related complications, with $10k–15k incremental cost per hospitalization; despite industry-leading
products, up to 68% of patients still have major bleeding events, up to $61k spent per patient, a 7% risk of
mortality and a 70% chance of requiring transfusion. HemoMax is being developed toward an IDE as a class III PMA
vascular sealant.
From their own site: the adhesives are inspired by the mucus secreted by the Dusky Arion slug and are "composed of
a dual polymeric network"; they "stay tough and highly stretchable under mechanical stress, while bonding to wet
tissue within seconds", and rely on "physical entanglement and electrostatic interactions" instead of the toxic
chemical linkers used in many existing adhesives. Stated properties: tough adhesion >1000–2000 J/m²; stretch up to
20×; adhesion in under 30 seconds; hemostasis in under 30 seconds.

## Peer-reviewed publications (their publications page; verified on PubMed where cited)
- Kent DO, … Freedman BR, et al. "Hemostatic Tough Adhesives seal tissue and control hemorrhage."
  *Nature Communications*, 8 Apr 2026. PMID 41951623. (Their page lists it as "Hemorrhage Control | Kent &
  Freedman et al. 2026 Nature Communications": complete hemostasis in liver and spleen wounds in a preclinical
  porcine model of solid organ injury, outperforming leading commercial hemostatic products.)
- "Dural Repair | Wu & Freedman et al. 2024 Science Translational Medicine" — a bioadhesive hydrogel for
  sutureless dural sealing, with superior mechanical performance over commercial sealants in porcine and human
  ex vivo models.
- "Instant Adhesion | Freedman et al. 2024 PNAS" — dry chitosan films giving instant, tough adhesion
  (>3,000 J/m²) between hydrogels and elastomers via non-covalent interactions.
- "Topological Entanglement | Cintron-Cruz et al. 2022 Advanced Materials" — ultratough adhesion (>2000 J/m²)
  within minutes by topological entanglement of pH-responsive chitosan chains.
- "Tendon Repair | Freedman et al. 2021 Nature BME" — a dual-function hydrogel with an adhesive and a gliding
  surface, promoting tendon healing and reducing scar in a rat Achilles rupture model.
- "Degradable Hydrogel | Freedman et al. 2021 Advanced Materials"; "Bioadhesive Sutures | Ma et al. 2024
  Advanced Healthcare Materials"; "Hydrogel-coated Biomedical Plastics | Ma et al. 2024 Advanced Materials
  Technologies"; "Intraoral Hydrogel | Wu & Freedman et al. 2023 Journal of Dental Research" (DenTAl);
  "Tunable Drug Release | Koh & Freedman et al. 2022 Advanced Healthcare Materials";
  "Fetal Surgery | Lazow et al. 2020 Journal of Pediatric Surgery"; "Skin-Adhesive | Blacklow et al. 2019
  Science Advances".
- Their site states: "over 17 publications in prestigious research journals, accumulating over 6000 citations"
