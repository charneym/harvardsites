# Jillion Therapeutics Inc. — dossier (facts with sources)

## Company
- Brooklyn, NY: 760 Parkside Ave, Suite 310, Brooklyn, NY 11226. Email: welcome@jilliontherapeutics.com — https://www.jilliontherapeutics.com/
- Time zone: US Eastern.
- Positioning (site): "Translating world-class neuroscience into therapeutics"; projects supported by NIH; founder has 20+ years in a Nobel Prize-winning lab; bottom-up approach; accelerating preclinical discovery of CNS drug leads. — https://www.jilliontherapeutics.com/
- High-Power Screening (HPS) with DNA-encoded libraries (DELs): team coined "HPS" in Trends Pharmacol Sci 2022;43(1):4-15. DEL screens vast numbers of real compounds in a single tube within days; compounds made in 2–3 steps; DNA barcode decoded by NGS; in-house design of focused DEL libraries led by a Senior Chemist; A.I./ML for hit and lead optimization. — https://www.jilliontherapeutics.com/our-technology
- Vision: main brain "signaling highways" (dopaminergic, muscarinic, serotonergic, opioid) overexploited for 6–7 decades. Mission: HPS + in-house DEL to identify drug candidates for challenging neurological disorders. — https://www.jilliontherapeutics.com/about-us

## Funding (NIH RePORTER; PI Marc Flajolet on all)
- 2R44AG085856-02, NIA, SBIR Phase II, notice 2026-09-17, first-year $1,118,025: "Advancing DEL-derived Small Molecule Inhibitors of Pathological Tau Toward Preclinical Development for Alzheimer's Disease and Tauopathies". Phase I identified a large set of novel small molecules physically binding tau. Phase II: validate in biochemical and cell assays, medicinal chemistry, CNS exposure/safety, efficacy in tauopathy mouse models; nominate one lead + two backups for IND-enabling studies; framework for drugging intrinsically disordered proteins. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11407564
- 1R43AG085856-01A1 (NIA, 2024, $496,373): tau monomers/oligomers via DEL; tau expressed in mammalian cells; 125 million proprietary compounds. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11005644
- 1R43DA061688-01 (NIDA, 2024, $399,884): orphan GPCR GPR26 for opioid use disorders. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11005426
- 1R43DA062975-01 (NIDA, 2025, $399,483): AI + DEL polypharmacology, OPRD1 + DRD3 (+ HTR1A) for substance use disorders. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11142938
- 1R43DA061909-01 (NIDA, 2025, $398,093): orphan GPCR GPR37 for amphetamine use disorders. — https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11033982
- Grant text: "We have extensively studied these pathways, and the basal ganglia signaling in general, in the Greengard lab."
