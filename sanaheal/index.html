01 · The problem

Wet surfaces defeat glue.

Every wet surface carries a thin film of water. It sits between an adhesive and the tissue and blocks the contact that adhesion depends on. Conventional glues try to diffuse through it, so bonds come slowly and stay weak. Water beads, and wins.

Sources: sanaheal.com, “The Problem: Adhesion to Biological Tissues”; Yuk et al., Nature 2019.

02 · Dry the interface

The film lands and draws the water away.

SanaHeal's material arrives dry. Pressed gently onto wet tissue, it repels contaminants and absorbs surface moisture, drying the interface in the instant of contact. The barrier is gone.

Sources: sanaheal.com/technology (“repels contaminants and absorbs surface moisture”); Nature 2019. Inspired by spider-web glue, which pulls interfacial water away.

03 · Bond

Bonds form across the interface.

As it takes up water, the material swells and forms physical and chemical bonds for a near-instant seal: hydrogen and electrostatic bonds first, then covalent bonds with the tissue's own amine groups.

04 · Hold

The seal holds as the surface flexes.

Organs breathe, pulse and stretch. A seal that is itself a soft, tough hydrogel moves with them instead of tearing out. In published preclinical work, the barnacle-inspired paste sealed injuries in seconds, independent of blood clotting.

Nature-inspired technology

Learning from nature to achieve rapid, robust tissue adhesion.

Barnacles

Clean and adhere to uneven and contaminated surfaces.

A lipid-rich matrix first cleans contaminants off the surface; adhesive proteins then crosslink to it. SanaHeal's haemostatic paste borrows the trick: an oil phase pushes blood aside so adhesive microparticles can bond.

Spider webs

Instantly dry and adhere to wet surfaces.

Web glue carries hygroscopic compounds that pull interfacial water away, so the silk sticks even in humid air. SanaHeal's dry patch does the same on wet tissue.

SanaHeal's materials are synthetic and plant-derived. Sources: sanaheal.com (home, technology); Yuk et al., Nat. Biomed. Eng. 2021 (barnacle glue); Nature 2019 (spider-web glue).

Bioadhesive paste

Rapid, coagulation-independent control of bleeding. Bioadhesive microparticles in a hydrophobic oil that repels blood, inspired by barnacle glue.

02 · Patch

Bioadhesive patch

Seals organ injuries against fluid and air leaks. A dry sheet that absorbs interfacial water and bonds under gentle pressure, inspired by spider-web glue.

The platform can also carry added functions, such as drug delivery and electrical conductivity.
