## Activity (newest first)
- **NIH NICHD STTR Phase I, 1R41HD122577-01**: "Novel Wearable Electrode Array Stimulation Technology for Post-Stroke Drop-Foot
  Assistance". Notice 10 Sep 2026; project 15 Sep 2026 – 14 Sep 2027; award $399,632. Contact PI Nitin Sharma; PI Xiangming Xue.
  https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11407287 . Also announced on sharmalabncsu.org lab news.
  Abstract: footdrop (insufficient ankle dorsiflexion in swing) is a highly prevalent post-stroke gait deficit; FES devices such as
  NESS L300 use single active electrode stimulation (SES), which can prematurely cause muscle fatigue and needs constant manual intensity
  adjustment ("a primary cause of device disuse"). iSDAS (interleaved spatially distributed asynchronous stimulation) uses multiple
  electrodes to asynchronously stimulate separate motor nerve branches in a muscle belly and nerve trunk, mimicking physiological firing,
  to delay or avoid FES-induced fatigue. Challenge: electrode placement during donning. Aim 1: GUIDE – Guided Ultrasound for Intelligent
  Dynamic Electrodes, automatically determines personalized electrode locations. Aim 2: fabricate an ankle assistance neural sleeve with
  integrated wearable ultrasound sensors and an ultrasound-compatible electrode array.
- **Apr 2025**: 3rd place, Daugherty Endowment Track, 2025 VenturePack Challenge (NC State Entrepreneurship); $10,000 prize. Project:
  "Wearable Ultrasound-Guided Afferent Stimulation Sleeve for Tremor Suppression" (body-conformable ultrasound sensors + stimulation
  electrodes in a textile wearable; Essential Tremor, Parkinson's). Team: Xiangming (Xue) and MyoMech.
  https://mae.ncsu.edu/jiang/news/2025/apr-2025-xiangming-and-myomech-awarded-3rd-place-in-2025-venturepack-challenge

## Company copy (site)
- "Enable Mobility with Wearable Muscle Imaging"; "We create devices for rehabilitation that can be worn underneath clothes".
- 5.4 million people in the US are mobility-impaired due to stroke, spinal cord injuries, multiple sclerosis and cerebral palsy;
  rehabilitation costs ~$34B; average daily cost $716 to $991 per patient.
- Developing wearable ultrasound sensors to detect muscle fatigue, integrated into rehab devices for people with lower limb disabilities.
- Neural sleeve: garment over one leg, ankle to hip; embedded US sensors and FES electrodes monitor muscle state and control closed-loop
  FES in quadriceps, hamstrings, gastrocnemius and ankle dorsiflexor. Image processing algorithm detects muscle length, thickness and
  strain changes due to FES-induced fatigue or user intent ("a great improvement over EMG sensors"). Feedback proactively modulates FES
  for synchronized, fatigue-resistant responses. Hip-mounted electronics (device mockup image).
- Vision: existing under-clothing devices (WalkAide, Bioness L300) use FES on one muscle group and do not image muscles. Phone app for
  people with disabilities, medical professionals and athletes.
