## Science (sourced)
NSF 2535594 abstract: "force-stimulated delivery platform ... utilizes mechano-activated microcapsules combined with
the natural mechanical forces of the joint for the controlled and directed delivery of therapeutics"; "compatible with a
wide variety of compounds"; Phase II covers "a scalable manufacturing process" retaining microcapsule quality and
reproducibility, release kinetics, and "A toxicology study ... to confirm the safety and localization profile".
"Bioavailability within the joint tends to be poor".
NSF 2304235 abstract: "uses the body's natural physiological loading of musculoskeletal environments for controlled
release of nearly any drug"; "tunable rupture profile of proprietary mechano-activated microcapsules"; preliminary work
showed the capsules "encapsulate and release viable biological therapeutics upon mechanical force", "tunable
mechano-activation thresholds", and "stay and rupture within a living joint". Musculoskeletal conditions "impact 1.71
billion people globally". Drugs: "small molecules, proteins, and biologics".
NSF 2304235 project outcomes report (G. Dodge, 14 Mar 2025): IL-1Ra encapsulated via "proprietary microfluidic
encapsulation technique"; randomized controlled study in an established equine model of IL-1β-induced joint
inflammation vs soluble IL-1Ra and empty MAMCs; "No test groups showed any adverse or toxic effects from the empty or
full MAMCs"; "the MAMC IL-1Ra group demonstrated superior performance in reducing animal lameness and modulating the
inflammatory response"; "clinical efficacy was maintained at 3 weeks after a single injection".
NIH 1R43AR087914 abstract: first product under development manages joint pain and inflammation after acute trauma or
surgery: low-dose local anesthetic (bupivacaine) + NSAID (ketorolac) in MAMCs, single intra-articular injection at the
time of surgery; goal ≥72 h of relief, "at least 3X longer than the current standard of post-operative care (72 h vs.
24 h)"; "different mechano-activation thresholds can be achieved by altering MAMC shell thickness and size"; proposed
bioadhesive polydopamine (PDA) coating to keep MAMCs where placed. Aim 1: release kinetics and distribution with/without
PDA in a porcine ex vivo joint. Aim 2: distribution, toxicity, efficacy in an acute joint pain rat model.
Company site: microcapsules made by "proprietary microfluidic technology and double emulsion process"; "encapsulate and
release nearly any soluble drug"; "size-controlled microcapsules at a range of 50+ microns"; "PLGA, an FDA-approved
biodegradable polymer for over 30 years". Advantages listed: targeted & controlled local release; stimuli-responsive
activation ('on demand'); dose sparing; sustained bioavailability.
