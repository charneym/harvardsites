# Xosomix (slug: xosomix)

Facts only, each with its source. Retrieved 2026-11-19.

## Company (source: https://xosomix.com/ , a single-page site)
- "Xosomix (pronounced as "exosomix") is a biotechnology company involved in the development of the next generation therapeutics using exosomes."
- "Xosomix is founded on the preclinical research demonstrating the first therapeutic use of exosomes in a brain disorder, Rett Syndrome."
- Tagline: "Next generation therapeutics based on exosomes."
- Problem (site): about 20% of the US population suffers from a diagnosable mental illness; brain disorder therapeutics is a ~$100B market with just 50% patient reach; current drugs "range from ineffective to partially effective and treat symptoms rather than altering the course of disease."
- Solution (site): "Exosomes are transportation and logistics services of the body that carry biomolecules to specific cellular targets and have capability to heal the defective cells and tissues. We harness the targeting capability of exosomes to improve the efficacy of current drugs and healing capability of exosomes to usher a new era of therapeutics for the brain disorders."
- Stats on site: 170 billion cells, 86 billion neurons in the human brain; 1 quadrillion synapses estimated in the human brain.
- "Xosomix is a resident of biotech incubator JLABS by Johnson & Johnson Innovation."
- Address: 3210 Merryfield Row, San Diego, CA 92121. Phone (858) 242-1576. Email info@xosomix.com. Legal name: Xosomix, LLC (©2020).
- Brand: logo = cyan double helix (#00aeef) inside a ring of paired figures (pink #d47eab, indigo #545fab, green #7fc155, teal, orange, red); wordmark "Xosomix" in #00aeef. Site accents #4da8ff, #52bad5. Site background image: neurons_circuit01.jpg (neuron culture micrograph).

## Programmes
- Rett syndrome therapeutics, preclinical (Synapse/Patsnap lists highest phase preclinical, indication Rett syndrome: https://synapse.patsnap.com/drug/ca8e5c3deb0945509d28d2139bd94622 ; BioSpace 2022 release: "therapeutic pipeline for Rett Syndrome").
- Alzheimer's disease: NIH NIA SBIR Phase I 1R43AG102280-01, "Extracellular Vesicle encapsulated Adeno-associated virus (EV-AAV)-mediated delivery of Neurotrophic factor-α1 (NF-α1)/Carboxypeptidase E (CPE) gene (EV-AAV-CPE) as a therapy for Alzheimer's Disease"; award notice 2026-09-17; project 2026-09-17 to 2028-02-29; FY2026 $349,359; PI Pranav Sharma. Abstract: AAV-CPE prevents and reverses memory impairment and pathology in AD mouse models; most AAV serotypes do not efficiently cross the BBB; EV-loaded AAV offers better BBB penetration, immune evasion, enhanced tropism and reduced toxicity. Aims: production of EV-AAV-CPE, QC/purity, safety and efficacy in patient iPSC-derived neuronal models. https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11410609
- Earlier: CRADA with the NIH (Dr. Peng Loh) to develop an Alzheimer's treatment (BioSpace, 2022-10-04).
- Endometriosis diagnostic: NICHD SBIR Phase I 1R43HD109099-01A1, "Quantitative proteomic profiling of extracellular vesicles from menstrual effluent for the discovery of non-invasive diagnostic biomarkers of endometriosis"; 2022-09-19 to 2024-08-31; $309,897; PI Pranav Sharma. https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/10547052 . Collaborator: Dr. Sanjay Agarwal, MD, UC San Diego Health (BioSpace).
- Platform: proprietary platform for analysing exosome signals, previously used to find a biomarker cohort for neural dysfunction in Rett syndrome (BioSpace 2022 release: https://www.biospace.com/xosomix-awarded-nih-phase-i-sbir-grant-to-develop-diagnostic-biomarker-for-endometriosis).

## Science (founding research)
- Sharma P. et al., "Exosomes regulate neurogenesis and circuit assembly," PNAS 2019 (Cline lab, Scripps Research). Exosomes from healthy (control) neurons rescued deficits in neuronal proliferation, differentiation, synaptogenesis and synchronized firing in MECP2-knockdown neural cultures; exosomes from MECP2-deficient cultures lacked this capability; exosome treatment increased proliferation in vivo in the dentate gyrus of P4 mice. Scripps news 2019-07-19: https://www.scripps.edu/news-events/news/20190719-cline-exosome/
