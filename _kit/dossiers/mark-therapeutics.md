## Science (quoted/paraphrased from the RePORTER abstracts for the awards above)
- Idiopathic Parkinson's disease (idPD) affects over 1% of the aging population. idPD patients are approximately 80% of all Parkinson's cases and have no family history or genetic mutations that explain the disease. No cure or disease-modifying therapy exists.
- MarkTx develops a first-in-class disease-modifying gene therapy using Adeno-Associated Viral (AAV) gene therapy tailored to the specific cellular deficit found in idPD patients: it compensates for the pathogenic (epigenetic) loss of expression of the PLA2g6L protein, restores critical cellular functions in dopaminergic (DA) neurons and halts their pathological loss in brain regions responsible for motor function.
- Mark101, the first experimental candidate: proof of concept for efficacy and safety; one-time AAV-driven expression of functional PLA2g6L protein stopped pathological loss of DA neurons in the substantia nigra and significantly slowed progression of motor dysfunction in a preclinical idPD-like aging mouse model.
- Intellectual property: four issued patents and an exclusive licence from Boston University.
- Phase I aims: optimise and test new AAV candidate constructs; in vitro validation of therapeutic potential in human DA neurons; in vivo optimisation and validation of PLA2g6L delivery to DA neurons in live animals.
- Phase II, lead candidate Mark202. Aim 1: optimise and validate idPD-like mouse models representing progressive age-dependent pathology, focusing on MarkTx's PFF-enhanced hybrid model. Aim 2: validate efficacy and safety of Mark202 in idPD-like aging and PFF-enhanced hybrid mouse models at early and later stages — (a) stop pathological loss of DA neurons in the SNc, (b) slow progression of motor dysfunction, (c) demonstrate efficacy against other pathological hallmarks of idPD. Aim 3: preclinical testing in a non-human primate (NHP) model of PD.
- Underlying publication: Zhou Q, Yen A, Rymarczyk G, Asai H, Trengrove C, Aziz N, Kirber MT, Mostoslavsky G, Ikezu T, Wolozin B, Bolotina VM. "Impairment of PARK14-dependent Ca2+ signalling is a novel determinant of Parkinson's disease." Nat Commun 2016;7:10332 — https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms10332 . Cells from idPD patients show significant deficiency in store-operated PLA2g6-dependent Ca2+ signalling; genetic or molecular impairment of that signalling triggers autophagic dysfunction, progressive loss of DA neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta and age-dependent L-DOPA-sensitive motor dysfunction. PubMed 26755131.
