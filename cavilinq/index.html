Quantum processors,linked by light.

CavilinQ builds cavity-enhanced photonic links that let individual quantum processors operate together as modular, high-performance clusters.

01 · Limit

One chip can onlyget so big.

Quantum computers have reached remarkable milestones, but they struggle to scale beyond the limits of a single processor. Modularity splits the problem in two: engineering within a module, and engineering between modules.

02 · Emit

Two mirrors, one atom,one mode.

In free space an atom's light goes in every direction, and a lens catches only part of it. Between the curved mirrors of an optical cavity, the atom–light interaction is enhanced and the photon leaves in a single, well-defined mode that couples into fiber.

CavilinQ's patented hardware: compact, ultra-low-loss photonic interfaces for direct QPU integration. Buckled-micromirror IP licensed from Harvard.

03 · Cross

The photon crossesto a second module.

The photon carries information about the atom's state. It travels through fiber to a second module and interacts with the atom in that module's cavity.

04 · Herald

A detection heraldsentanglement.

Detecting the photon signals success: two atoms in different modules now share one entangled state. The atoms can then be moved into memory, freeing each cavity for the next link.

Simplified for illustration; one of several possible protocols. Basis: Science 2021; Science 2025.

05 · Scale

Grow byadding modules.

High-fidelity, high-rate interconnects let systems scale by adding modules: a path toward fault-tolerant quantum computers and quantum data centers. Platform-agnostic by design, starting with neutral-atom processors.

“We are building the interconnects that unify isolated processors into one distributed processor.”

Shankar G. Menon, CEO · The Quantum Insider, April 2026

Platform01

One interconnect layer,many machines.

The same links that join processors can inject quantum information directly into them. CavilinQ will first demonstrate integration with neutral-atom quantum processors.

  1. A

    Distributed quantum computing

    Separate modules operating as one machine, toward fault-tolerant systems and quantum data centers.

  2. B

    Quantum-enhanced sensing

    Quantum information delivered into processors and sensors over photonic links.

  3. C

    Blind quantum computing

    Computation on a remote processor, enabled by injecting quantum information through the link.

Science02

Built on publishedlight–matter research.

CavilinQ's approach builds on work by its scientific co-founders, Mikhail Lukin (Harvard University) and Hannes Bernien (University of Chicago / University of Innsbruck), and on the founding team's own research.

  • 2021Science

    Entanglement transport and a nanophotonic interface for atoms in optical tweezers

    T. Đorđević, P. Samutpraphoot, P. L. Ocola, H. Bernien, B. Grinkemeyer, I. Dimitrova, V. Vuletić, M. D. Lukin

    Two atoms entangled through a photonic-crystal cavity, then moved out into free space with the entanglement preserved.
    doi:10.1126/science.abi9917

    An integrated atom array–nanophotonic chip platform with background-free imaging

    S. G. Menon, N. Glachman, M. Pompili, A. Dibos, H. Bernien

    Up to 64 tweezers next to a chip with more than 100 nanophotonic cavities; background-free imaging near the cavities at about 99.2% fidelity.
