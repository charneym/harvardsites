## Science / mechanism (RePORTER abstracts + LSI '23 transcript)
- Mild vacuum in the flashback chamber; on entering the vein and sensing the change in pressure, it deploys the soft gel IV catheter over the needle and into the vein via a simple spring mechanism, preventing double-wall penetration.
- Workflow (LSI '23): press a button to activate a vacuum in the back of the housing; under the skin the skin seals the vacuum around the needle tip; advance with standard technique; on entering the vein, blood withdrawn into the needle breaks the vacuum; the spring advances the soft catheter.
- Sensor can be incorporated into current PIVC devices at equivalent cost.
- Bench: response time 0.23 s vs 1.44 s for market predicates (Phase II abstract).
- In vivo: first-attempt success rate +122%; novice operators >250% (Phase II abstract).
- Adult variant: 98% first-attempt success in vivo (Phase I abstracts).
- Phase II aims: design-control-level verification (Aim 1); GLP safety studies and human factors assessment to support 510(k) submission (Aim 2).
- Problem stats (abstracts): >330M PIVCs/yr in US, 1.2B globally; pediatric first-attempt success 23%; infiltration/extravasation >50% of children and infants; failed first attempt ~5-fold greater chance of failure in subsequent placements.
- LSI '23: 33M US hospitalizations/yr, ~90% receive an IV catheter, ~50% (168M) fail; failure modes infiltration, phlebitis, occlusion; core mechanism protected by US and world issued patents; average catheters per patient 1.7 → 1.2 (company estimate).
- Site: "US and WTO Patents Issued", "Integration with current PIVCs", "Simple core mechanism"; pillars Operator Skill, Visualization, Assist Devices, Complications.
