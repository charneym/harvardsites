## Mission (homepage, verbatim gist)
"Advance the treatment of peripheral arterial disease (PAD) through the development of next-generation endovascular platform technology that addresses critical gaps in current vascular interventions." Initial focus: stenotic femoropopliteal arteries with a high degree of bending and tortuosity. Device engineered to minimize and delay restenosis; flexible, scalable design intended to expand into additional vascular applications and support physicians in delivering more durable, procedure-efficient care.

## Technology: Biocarpet (technology page)
Flexible, conformable, bioresorbable scaffold for peripheral vascular disease. Partial, direct barrier to pathological cell migration; minimizes disruption to blood flow through patient-specific conformability; supports biomechanical homeostasis by allowing low-pressure deployment with minimal arterial overstretch; as it resorbs, reduces the chronic inflammatory response.
Five steps on the site's figure: 1 Insertion · 2 Low-pressure balloon inflation · 3 Thermoforming · 4 Cooling, scaffold formation · 5 Catheter withdrawal, bioresorption.

## NIH SBIR (latest activity)
- 1R44HL184700-01, "Development and Chronic Preclinical Assessment of a Flexible Endovascular Scaffold for Treating Stenosis in the Femoropopliteal Arterial Segments". PI Ali Behrangzade. FY2026 award $918,166; notice 2026-09-17; project 2026-09-20 to 2028-08-31. https://reporter.nih.gov/project-details/11313771
- Abstract facts: >3 million Americans estimated to have advanced PAD; two-year patency 50% (angioplasty) and 60% (drug-eluting balloons); >70% of patients presenting with advanced lower-extremity PAD affected in femoral and popliteal arteries, where bending leads to stent fracture and restenosis. Biocarpet: flat sheet rolled around an uninflated thermal balloon catheter (TBC); TBC inflates to unfurl it into the vessel's shape and thermoform it in situ so adjacent layers attach; heat off, cools to body temperature and locks; TBC deflated and removed; scaffold resorbs as the artery remodels to stay patent. Prior NIH R61 data (design/manufacturing, in-vitro mechanics, ex-vivo thermal safety, deliverability incl. deceased porcine model). Aims: pass ASTM in-vitro standards; 6-month in-vivo degradation and performance in healthy porcine model; foundation for future GLP animal trials.
