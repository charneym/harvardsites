## Company
- PatchClamp Medtech, Inc. (PMI): a medical device start-up in Seattle, WA (RePORTER: Seattle, WA 98112).
- Device: a dural repair device for endoscopic spine surgery and minimally invasive (MIS) cranial procedures, also usable in open surgery. A low-profile, single-hand disposable applicator ("about the width of a drinking straw", Design News) delivers a tissue or synthetic graft through a narrow MIS access port. The graft, with support ribs folded inside a restraining cylinder, passes through the dural perforation and re-expands on the inner surface of the dura. A bioabsorbable clasp (external struts folded into a cylinder) is released onto the outer dural surface to hold it. It can be unclasped and repositioned. Goal: an immediate watertight seal.
- Why: CSF pressure is higher than atmospheric, so any opening in the dura leaks persistently (Mayberg, Design News). Leaks lead to infection, meningitis, intractable headaches and wound breakdown. Most MIS cases run at a depth of about 8–10 cm through an 8–24 mm port (Mayberg, Design News).
- Phase I aims: in vitro burst-pressure chamber tests, constrained-access lumbar simulators and a pressurized spine laminotomy model, and an acute pig laminectomy durotomy model in vivo. The Phase II abstract says Phase I "confirmed the feasibility", with "substantial improvements in sealing time and effectiveness compared to traditional methods".
- Phase II: scale-up manufacturing, design enhancements, GLP biocompatibility studies, and regulatory and pre-submission work toward an anticipated 510(k) clearance.
- Test rig (Design News): CSF pressures during surgery are typically 0 to 35 cm of water, normal 7 to 18.
- Purpose (site): "an affordable and reliable technology for repairing and sealing durotomies". Impact: reduce complications, improve outcomes, lower hospital costs, decrease hospital readmissions, shorten OR time.
- IP (site): a patent filed and received as of January 2020; USPTO utility patent with Prioritized Patent Examination approved as of January 2021; international applications in 2022; IP protection in Japan. All base IP is assigned to PMI.
- Mayberg: 40 years as a neurosurgeon and more than 3,000 operations led to the idea (site).
- Not FDA approved (Newswire disclaimer). The site marks it as investigational.
