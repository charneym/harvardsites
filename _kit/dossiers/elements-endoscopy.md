## Science (Phase II abstract, paraphrase / quotes)
- US projected deficit of 1,630 gastroenterologists in 2025; additional 19 million patients entering the screening pool.
- Colonoscope design "unchanged since the 1980s"; 67.9% of endoscopists suffer injuries; competence requires 200–400 procedures; repairs average $5,833 per incident; mechanical cable-actuated systems create inherent limitations.
- Drive-by-wire colonoscope replaces mechanical cable-actuated systems with electronic control interfaces and drives; modular design, consistent performance.
- Phase I delivered a complete functional prototype validating electromechanical/hydraulic actuation, haptic feedback controls and modular architecture.
- Phase II aims: (1) final electromechanical/hydraulically-based tip deflection subsystem, >200-cycle functional lifetime; (2) refined ergonomic handle for diverse hand sizes, >20,000-cycle durability; (3) separable connections with IP68-sealed hydraulic interfaces and validated disinfection efficacy; (4) full modular system assessed with gastroenterologists using standardized protocols.
- Test targets: force accuracy ±0.05 N, motion resolution ±0.5 mm, performance within 5% of Olympus PCF-H190D specifications; clinician assessments across 5th–95th percentile hand sizes; user acceptance >80%.
- "first successful implementation of drive-by-wire architecture in flexible endoscopy". Expected outcome: design-frozen system ready for FDA 510(k) preparation; equivalent per-procedure costs without maintenance burden; consistent performance over lifecycle; physician-specific ergonomic customization; data collection enabling future AI-driven procedural assistance.
- Phase I abstract: reusable endoscopes cleaned by high level disinfection; reports show insufficient cleaning and infection transmission; disposable scopes usually inferior in manipulability and image quality. Approach: separate the handle from the flexible portion; flexible portion single use; reusable handle houses actuators connected to pull wires; software matches handling of preferred reusable scopes; duodenoscope and colonoscope bodies; tested with physicians in phantom models.
