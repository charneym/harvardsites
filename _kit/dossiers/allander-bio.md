## Science facts used
- Tat-PYC-Smad7: truncated Smad7 (PY motif + C-terminal MH2) fused to HIV-1 Tat protein transduction domain (Boss et al.,
  Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys 2021, doi 10.1016/j.ijrobp.2021.09.039; SBIR abstract). In a dog model of radiation-induced oral
  mucositis the topical gel mainly penetrated epithelial cells, was undetectable in serum, shortened grade 3 mucositis, and
  lowered IL-1β and TNF-α (same paper, PMC8810686).
- 2026 paper "Smad7-based biologic targeting epidermis and stroma promotes healing of diabetic wounds in mice and pigs"
  (preprint bioRxiv 10.64898/2025.12.31.697220; Allander news 2026-03-27): TGFβ and NFκB activated in human diabetic wounds;
  Smad7 as a TGFβ/NFκB dual inhibitor; topical Tat-PYC-Smad7 accelerated healing in diabetic mice and pigs; keratinocytes and
  neutrophils the main target cells. The page does not name the journal.
- Clin Cancer Res 2018 (doi 10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-18-1081): Smad7 promotes healing of RT-induced oral mucositis without
  compromising oral cancer therapy in a xenograft model. Nat Med 2013 (nm.3118). JID 2023 (IL22RA2, doi 10.1016/j.jid.2023.04.029).
- Funding (site news + RePORTER): NIH SBIR Phase II $1.5M 2017 (oral mucositis, DE024659); second Phase II $1.5M 2018 (chronic
  wounds incl. diabetic, DE028718); Colorado Advanced Industries award $250,000 (2020-05-23); NIH SBIR Phase II $1.68M 2020
  (radiation dermatitis, AR078669); SBIR-CRP $2.48M 2023 (IND-enabling GLP systemic toxicology, SB1DE024659); NCI SBIR Phase II
  $2.4M 2025 (first-in-human trial of oral gel, CA310522); NIDCR 2026 (cGMP clinical batch, DE034643). "6 NIH SBIR awards since
  2017" = the six distinct Phase II-level projects above (DE024659-02, DE028718, AR078669, SB1DE024659-04, CA310522, DE034643).
- "3 species": mice, dogs (oral mucositis 2021; radiodermatitis 2025 canine + mouse), pigs (diabetic wounds 2026).
