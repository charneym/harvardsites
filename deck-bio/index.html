Expanding the reach of T cell engagers into solid tumors.

Deck Bio builds T cell engagers on a stabilized T cell receptor. One engineered binder reads several tumor-only peptide–MHC targets, so more patients may qualify and tumors have fewer ways to escape.

01One target

A single target leaves most patients out.

Bet on one peptide–MHC and only some tumors show enough of it to clear the bar. Silence that one protein and the tumor escapes.

02Several targets, one binder

One binder reads several tumor-only targets.

Deck Bio chooses signatures of proteins that yield identical or highly similar peptides on the same HLA allele, HLA-A*02:01. One engineered binder recognizes them all. The proteins are regulated independently, so escape takes several losses, not one.

03Healthy cells passed over

Healthy tissue is read, and passed over.

dbSCOPE tests binders against the peptides that healthy tissues actually display: 13,849 for HLA-A*02:01. DBXO-1 showed more than 1,000-fold EC50 selectivity for its targets over off-target peptides.

A stabilized, soluble T cell receptor (dbTv) holds the stacked targets. The engager's other arm recruits a T cell, which forms a synapse with the tumor cell and kills it.

The problem

T cell engagers have shown clinical efficacy in blood cancers. In solid tumors, scarce and uneven targets have kept them to niche indications.

Inside out

Cells chop their own proteins into peptides and display them on MHC molecules. That lets a binder read intracellular cancer proteins and pick targets that only tumors express.

Precedent

Tebentafusp (Kimmtrak), approved by the FDA in 2022 for uveal melanoma, also targets a peptide–MHC.

Approach

Stack the odds, one card at a time.

01

Read the inside of the cell

Peptide–MHC turns intracellular cancer proteins into surface targets a binder can recognize.

02

Choose a signature

Several tumor-exclusive proteins that yield identical or highly similar peptides on HLA-A*02:01, so one drug can recognize them all.

03

Raise the copy number

Stacking targets raises surface copies, so more patients meet the expression threshold.

NSCLC: >2× patients vs a single popular pMHC target (company estimate)

04

Check every card

dbSCOPE screens binders against peptides that healthy tissues display, to catch off-target risk early.

Platform

Three parts, one hand.

Each part of the platform addresses a different reason TCR-based drugs have been hard to make.

dbTv

Stabilized TCR domain

The binder. A proprietary stabilization technology brings soluble TCR properties closer to those of monoclonal antibodies.

May lower manufacturing risk, and could enable subcutaneous dosing if the data support it.

dbTCE

T cell engager format

Engages peptide–MHC through the stabilized soluble TCR instead of a TCR-mimetic antibody fragment.

Designed for antibody-like stability and manufacturability; the other arm recruits the T cell.

dbSCOPE

Specificity profiling

A sequence-agnostic workflow that tests binder reactivity against peptides displayed in healthy tissues for a given HLA type.

For DBXO-1: 13,849 HLA-A*02:01 healthy-tissue peptides, with an off-target profile comparable to an approved pMHC T cell engager.

Lead program · Preclinical

DBXO-1. One binder, major solid tumors.

A multi-pMHC targeted bispecific T cell engager. A single engineered TCR-based binder recognizes several cancer-associated peptide–MHC complexes on HLA-A*02:01, aiming to widen patient eligibility and make responses more durable.

  • Non-small cell lung cancer Initial focus
  • Gastroesophageal cancer Initial focus
  • Head & neck cancer Signature expressed
  • Liver cancer Signature expressed

In these tumor types, about 30–50% of patients express the target signature at sufficient levels, and the tumors are relatively well immune-infiltrated. Clinical focus is on biomarker-selected patients.
