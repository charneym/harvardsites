## Facts used (tearsolutions.com unless noted)
- Tear film: lipid, aqueous and mucin layers and their roles; low/no lacritin → tear film instability, pro-inflammatory cytokines, cell damage, neural signalling disruption; with lacritin/Lacripep → balance of layers, cell repair, neural sensing (home page "The Tear Film").
- Lacritin discovered and named by Dr. Gordon Laurie's UVA lab in an unbiased screen begun in 1992; Lacripep a synthetic peptide embodiment of all known lacritin activities (bio). NIH National Eye Institute funding; 119 AA protein; dual neurotrophic and prosecretory functions (June 2026 release).
- Preclinical: restored basal tearing without irritation and healed the corneal surface. FIH Phase I/II in primary Sjögren's (NCT03226444), ~35 clinics in 17 states, statistically significant improvement in sign and symptom at two weeks. Phase IIb in moderate/severe dry eye planned at ~10–12 clinics.
- Stats: ~70M people in the US with signs and/or symptoms of dry eye; ~17M diagnosed in the US (home page counters).
- IP exclusively licensed from UVA Licensing and Ventures Group (Nov 2024 release). Investigational-drug notice from the site footer.

## Story
One fine-line cross-section of the eye's surface: lipid, aqueous and mucin layers over the corneal epithelium (squamous, wing, basal cells), nerves and stroma. Step 1 lacritin (rose) in the aqueous layer; step 2 lacritin fades, the lipid layer breaks up, surface cells inflame, nerve endings dim; step 3 Lacripep drops fall and spread, the film closes, the cornea calms and the nerve endings return.
