01 · Structure

An MRI shows structure.Not chemistry.

A conventional MRI image is built from the strong signal of water and fat. The metabolites that reveal what tissue is actually doing are present at concentrations roughly 10,000 times lower, so they never appear in the picture.

02 · Voxel

Select one voxel.

A spectroscopy voxel is a small cube of tissue placed on the anatomical scan. Everything that follows is the chemistry of this one cube.

The normative data published by BrainSpec’s scientific co‑founder used a 2 × 2 × 2 cm (8 cm³) voxel. Lin et al., NeuroRx 2005.

03 · Signal

Spins ring, then fade.

In the scanner’s 3‑tesla field, hydrogen nuclei align. A radio‑frequency pulse tips them; they precess near 127.7 MHz, each metabolite slightly shifted by the electrons around it, and the ringing signal decays. That fading echo is the free‑induction decay.

Precession is slowed about 10⁸ times and chemical‑shift beats are exaggerated so the eye can follow them.

04 · Spectrum

The wave unfolds intoa chemical fingerprint.

A Fourier transform turns the decay into peaks at characteristic positions in parts per million. Radiologists read ratios: a rising choline peak and a falling NAA peak, with lactate and lipid appearing, is the classic tumour pattern.

Peak positions and the tumour pattern follow Lin et al., NeuroRx 2005. Heights are illustrative ratios to creatine, not measurements.

Biochemical change appears beforestructural change. BrainSpec makes itmeasurable on the scanner the hospitalalready has.

Magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) reads the faint signal of brain metabolites from the same MRI scanner, in the same session, with no ionizing radiation and no surgery. It is often described as a virtual biopsy.

BrainSpec makes the result as simple to read as routine blood test results, with applications in brain tumours, Alzheimer’s disease, multiple sclerosis and more.

BrainSpec Core™

Spectroscopy as simpleto read as a blood test.

A web‑based platform that pairs a post‑processing back end, built on a linear‑combinations model of the spectrum, with an interface made for clinicians and researchers. Everything is processed in the cloud, so results are always up to date.

Quantified neurometabolite concentrations from a linear‑combinations model fit, compared against a reference database of brain chemistry.

What MRS measures

Eight neurometabolites,one spectrum.

The metabolites BrainSpec describes on its science page, with the molecule drawings from that page.

NAA

N‑Acetylaspartate

2.0 ppm

Marker of neuronal health, used in tumours, head injury and dementia. Elevated in recovery and in Canavan disease.

Cho

Choline

3.2 ppm

Linked to cell‑membrane turnover and cell density; relevant to tumours and other proliferative conditions.

Cr

Creatine

3.0 ppm

Central energy marker, relatively stable, often used as the internal reference for ratios.

Glu

Glutamate

2.1–2.4

Central to neurotransmission; changes may indicate neurological conditions or altered synaptic activity.

Gln

Glutamine

2.1–2.4

Tied to amino‑acid and neurotransmitter processes; changes may signal neurodegeneration or pathway disruption.

mI

myo‑Inositol

3.6 ppm

Involved in osmoregulation and signal transduction; changes may reflect cell stress, volume change or neuroinflammation.

Lac

Lactate

1.33 ppm

Marker of anaerobic metabolism, seen in hypoxia, ischemia and some tumours.

2‑HG

2‑Hydroxyglutarate

≈2.25 ppm

Oncometabolite relevant to IDH‑mutant glioma; can be used to track tumour progression.

How it is read

The output of MRS is a spectrum of resonance peaks in parts per million, not an image. In vivo ¹H‑MRS examines roughly the 1–5 ppm range, and ratios between metabolites help diagnose and monitor disease.

Our story

The team formed at a brainstorming session hosted by the iHub at Brigham and Women’s Hospital, a program created to help clinicians and researchers turn ideas into reality. Collaborators include radiologists, oncologists, neurologists and neurosurgeons at Brigham and Women’s and Massachusetts General Hospital.

Our mission

To revolutionize neuroimaging through software that improves the accuracy, accessibility and clinical usefulness of MRS data, so clinicians and researchers can make informed decisions about patient care.

Our vision

To put the power of MRS into the hands of clinicians and researchers worldwide: turning complex neuroimaging data into actionable insight, advancing MRS research, and improving the lives of people with neurological conditions.
