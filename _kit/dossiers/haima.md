## Science (https://www.haimatherapeutics.com/platform and /synthoplate)
- "Platelets are small, anuclear cells that circulate in the blood, considered by many to be the body's natural drug delivery vehicle. Their primary role is to stop bleeding after injury by regulating the process of clotting (called hemostasis)."
- Natural platelet mechanism, as described: platelets "bind to the injury site through interaction of their surface receptors with injury site-specific proteins, like collagen and von Willebrand Factor"; after adhesion they "activate and change their shape and expression of other surface receptors, like GPIIb-IIIa"; "Upon activation, platelets become cross-linked through binding of blood protein fibrinogen"; "stabilization of the clot is achieved through the formation of a fibrin mesh."
- Platform: "Haima's platelet-inspired platform technology leverages these surface interactions and bioactive cargo delivery on a nanoparticle platform." Areas listed: hemostasis, thrombosis, thromboinflammation, cancer metastasis.
- Stated advantages over cell-based products: "Tunable and specific activity (no off-target activity)"; "Made from synthetic materials (no biological sourcing)"; "Easy and economical to manufacture at large scale"; "Long shelf-life (reduced waste from product expiry)"; "Portable (use outside of large hospital systems and cold-chain storage)"; "No type-matching or biologic immune response".
- SynthoPlate: "an IV-injectable hemostatic agent that mimics and amplifies platelet's adhesion and aggregation functions. SynthoPlate is fully synthetic, available for large scale manufacture, easily portable, and requires no need for blood type matching."
- Donor-platelet limitations quoted by the company: "over 2 million units of donor platelets are transfused in the clinic to treat bleeding in the United States"; "20% of collected platelets being discarded"; "Short shelf-life (5-7 days after donation)"; donor dependence, special storage, type-matching, infection risk.
- Indications described: **Traumatic injury** — "Trauma is the #1 cause of death in people ages 1-44, and up to 40% trauma-related deaths are considered potentially preventable if bleeding can be controlled sooner after injury. Donor platelet products are currently not available for use before a trauma patient reaches the hospital." **Surgery** — "SynthoPlate can be used to control bleeding and minimize donor blood use before, during, and after complex surgeries." **Thrombocytopenia** — "Over 750,000 thrombocytopenia patients receive donor platelet transfusions annually in the US, and roughly 25% become refractory (non-responsive) to platelet transfusion."

## Peer-reviewed results (listed by the company with PubMed links)
- "Intraosseous administration of lyophilized synthetic platelets renders hemostatic efficacy in rat model of traumatic hemorrhage." *J Thromb Haemost* 2026. Company summary: "In a rat liver laceration model, intraosseous (IO) injection of lyophilized SynthoPlate (Lyo-SP) nanoparticles significantly reduced blood loss by ~56%, improved survival, and stabilized hemodynamics." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41224176/
- "Platelet-inspired synthetic nanoparticles improve hemostasis and hemodynamics in a rabbit model of abdominal hemorrhage." *Journal of Trauma and Acute Care Surgery* 2024. "SynthoPlate significantly reduced blood loss by ~36% and improved hemodynamics…" https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38057963/
- "Efficacy of platelet-inspired hemostatic nanoparticles on bleeding in von Willebrand disease murine models." *Blood* 2023. "SynthoPlate significantly improved clot formation and reduced blood loss by 28-65% in mouse models of von Willebrand disease types 2B and 3." https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36928925/
- "Intravenous synthetic platelet (SynthoPlate) nanoconstructs reduce bleeding and improve 'golden hour' survival in a porcine model of traumatic arterial hemorrhage." *Sci Rep* 2018. "SynthoPlate reduced blood loss by ~65%, stabilized blood pressure, and improved survival when administered within the 'golden hour'…" https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29449604/
- "In vitro characterization of SynthoPlate (synthetic platelet) technology and its in vivo evaluation in severely thrombocytopenic mice." *J Thromb Haemost* 2017. "…safely enhanced hemostasis and reduced bleeding time in a dose dependent manner by up to 62% in thrombocytopenic mice." https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27925685
- Earlier platform papers: *ACS Nano* 2014 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/25318048); *Nanoscale* 2014 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/24658160); *Biomaterials* 2013 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/23357371); *Bioconj Chem* 2012 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/22607514); *Biomacromolecules* 2012 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/22468641). The *Nanoscale* 2014 summary states: "A factor VIII-derived peptide enables von Willebrand factor (VWF)-binding of artificial platelet nanoconstructs without interfering with VWF-adhesion of natural platelets." The *Biomaterials* 2013 summary states the construct combines "CBP, VBP, and FMP" peptide motifs on liposomes.

## Funding (NIH RePORTER API; first-year award amounts)
- **1SB1HL184353-01**, NHLBI, award notice 2026-09-17, $499,999 — "Commercialization Strategy for an Intravenous Hemostatic Nanotechnology for Bleeding Management" (SBIR Commercialization Readiness Pilot). PI: Michael Arthur Bruckman. Abstract: "uncontrolled bleeding leads to nearly half a million deaths globally each year"; objectives are "to support the development of a robust clinical and regulatory strategy for expeditious FDA approval (Aim 1) and of a pricing and reimbursement strategy for the SP product in the initial and subsequent clinical indications (Aim 2)"; the abstract records that in Phase II "SP demonstrated the ability reduce bleeding in a more clinically relevant model of TCP in rabbits, recovered hemostatic function in TCP patient samples using several ex vivo biomarker assays, and demonstrated acceptable safety and PK profile in healthy and TCP rabbits", with further models including "tail transection models in mice with von Willebrand disease (VWD) and on anti-platelet therapy, liver laceration trauma/surgery models in rats, kidney incision trauma models after dilutional coagulopathy in rabbits, and polytrauma hemorrhage models in rabbits".
- **5R44HL180258-02**, NHLBI, 2026-08-04, $978,218, plus supplement 3R44HL180258-02S1, 2026-08-14, $50,000 — "A Novel Platelet-Inspired Nanotechnology to Restore Hemostasis in Patients on Antiplatelet Agents". PI: Ujjal Didar Singh Sekhon. (Phase I/II start: 1R44HL180258-01, 2025-07-11, $944,676.) Abstract: antiplatelet therapy with P2Y12 inhibitors "significantly increase the risk of bleeding complications, impacting over 1.4 million lives annually in the United States"; "10-31% of TBI patients [are] taking P2Y12 inhibitors prior to admission"; preliminary data show SP "reducing bleeding by 80%, and enhancing survival in preclinical models of P2Y12 inhibitor-induced hemorrhage".
- 2R44HL145948-02, NHLBI, 2023-06-01, $994,448; 5R44HL145948-03, 2024-05-20, $972,163; supplements 2024 ($112,804) and 2025 ($50,000) — "SynthoPlate: Intravenous Hemostatic Nanotechnology to Mitigate Bleeding in Thrombocytopenia". PI: Michael Arthur Bruckman.
