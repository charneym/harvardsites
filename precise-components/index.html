About us

CNC machining for the most complex components

We are a sub-contract CNC manufacturing business in Milton Keynes that frequently takes on the most complex of components, for sectors from medical and aviation to defence. Since our managing director took over in 2000, constant reinvestment in state-of-the-art machinery has changed the company beyond recognition, and his two sons have joined to make it a family run business.

  • 1966Precise Components is established and opens its first workshop in St Albans.
  • 1987The first CNC milling machine.
  • 2000Our current managing director takes over the business.
  • 2010Investment in automation: our first twin-spindle lathe with driven tooling.
  • 2019We move into our current premises in Milton Keynes.

How we work

From drawing to inspected part

  1. Design
  2. Turning
  3. Milling
  4. Inspection
  1. 01 · Design input

    Input on the design

    Customers tell us they come for our engineering input as much as our machining. We work with them on new designs to improve function and make parts cost effective, from a small new-product sample upward.

  2. 02 · Turning

    Done in one

    Every lathe we run is twin spindle with Y-axis milling, so parts are finished in one set-up: quicker, more accurate and without the surface marks that extra handling causes.

  3. 03 · Milling

    Lights-out production

    Our Mazak HCN-4000 horizontal machining centre has a 6-pallet loader for up to 72 workpieces, so ongoing orders run unattended. A 4-axis vertical machining centre handles lower-volume, high-mix work.

  4. 04 · Inspection

    A quality plan for every job

    We agree an inspection scheme with each client. Interval inspection documents are completed as the job progresses and are available to the customer for reference.

Millturn

Turning, done in one

Our approach to turning has always been ‘done in one’, reducing inaccuracies and surface imperfections caused by excessive part handling.

All our lathes are twin spindle with Y-axis milling, so parts are produced quicker, more accurately and with less set-up time.
