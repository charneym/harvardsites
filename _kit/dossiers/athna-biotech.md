## Science — AB-002 (bladder), verbatim from the 2R42CA262399-02 abstract
- "AB-002 consists of lipid-coated sodium chloride nanoparticles, and they are delivered intravesically and enter bladder cancer cells via endocytosis. Once internalized, the nanoparticles break down, releasing sodium and chloride ions, elevating intracellular solute concentrations, thereby inducing osmotic lysis of cancer cells."
- "AB-002 triggers immunogenic cell death and promotes antigen-specific immune responses, facilitating long-term tumor suppression."
- "Following treatment, the nanoparticles degrade into ions that are safely excreted in the urine, causing no cumulative or systemic toxicity."
- Need: bladder cancer "tenth most common cancer globally, with over 600,000 new cases and more than 200,000 deaths reported in 2022"; "Approximately 75% of cases are … NMIBC"; standard of care TURBT then intravesical BCG; "about one-third of patients do not respond to BCG, and an additional 10-15% are unable to tolerate it. For these patients, cystectomy is the recommended option".
- Phase I results: "developed a robust method to synthesize AB-002, demonstrated its ability to induce cancer cell death and activate immune responses, and showed that intravesically delivered AB-002 is well tolerated."
- Phase II aims: "establishing Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) manufacturing of AB-002, evaluating its efficacy in additional bladder cancer models, and conducting IND-enabling studies."
- Phase I abstract (1R43CA262399-01A1): "SCNPs are intravesically instilled into the affected bladder to kill residual cancer cells after TURBT."; "SCNPs essentially produce a vaccine in situ out of dying cancer cells".

## Science — AB-001 (lung), from the 5R42CA257584-03 abstract
- Radiation-responsive prodrug DM1-NO (nitrosylated DM1, a maytansinoid) loaded in PEG-PLGA nanoparticles conjugated with NTSmut, a ligand for NTSR1, "upregulated in 59.7% of lung tumors". Irradiation increases oxidative stress, releasing DM1 and nitric oxide, "Both DM1 and NO are potent radiosensitizers".
- Indication: unresectable stage III NSCLC, chemoradiotherapy toxicity.
- Phase I: "selective accumulation and sustained retention of AB-001 within tumors, enhanced tumor penetration, and superior efficacy in combination with radiotherapy compared to conventional treatments while minimizing toxic exposure to vital organs."
- Phase II: scale up manufacturing, GLP batch, additional murine models; "Following our Pre-IND meeting with the FDA, we will conduct IND-enabling toxicity evaluations and comprehensive pharmacokinetic studies".

## Publications (PubMed)
- Jiang W … Xie J. "NaCl Nanoparticles as a Cancer Therapeutic." Adv Mater 2019;31(46):e1904058. PMID 31553099. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31553099/
  "SCNPs but not salts are highly toxic to cancer cells. This is because SCNPs enter cells through endocytosis, bypassing cell regulations on ion transport. When dissolved inside cancer cells, SCNPs cause a surge of osmolarity and rapid cell lysis. Interestingly, normal cells are much more resistant to the treatment due to their relatively low sodium levels. Unlike conventional chemotherapeutics, SCNPs cause immunogenic cell death or ICD."
- Zhan S … Xie J. "Sodium Chloride Nanoparticles Potentiate Radiation Therapy by Disrupting Osmolarity Balance and Enhancing Antitumor Immunity." Nano Lett 2025;25(38):14032-14042. PMID 40947651. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40947651/
