Processes

Every process, one workshop

  1. 3D printing
  2. CNC
  3. Casting
  4. Bench
  5. Paint
  1. 01 · 3D printing

    SLA, SLS and FDM printing

    Parts are built layer by layer, straight from 3D CAD data. Ogle runs nine 3D printing machines, three SLS, five SLA and an FDM, so large and small jobs can be spread across the floor.

  2. 02 · CNC machining

    Machined in production materials

    Seven CNC machines with 3, 4 and 5-axis capability cut production-spec materials to tight tolerances, and refine 3D-printed parts to a superb surface finish.

  3. 03 · Vacuum casting

    Multiple copies from one master

    Ogle installed one of the first vacuum casting machines in the UK in 1988. From an accurate SLA master, parts are cast in rigid, flexible, clear or fire-retardant polymers.

  4. 04 · Modelmaking

    Traditional hand skills

    Several of Ogle's modelmakers served their apprenticeships as pattern makers at Vauxhall Motors, and those hand skills are passed on to the next generation.

  5. 05 · Paint & finish

    Finished in-house

    A colour-mixing room, a clean room holding thousands of colours and a 6.25 m paint booth mean every model gets its final finish under the same roof.

About Ogle

Modelmaking in Letchworth since 1954

David Ogle set the company up in 1954 after leaving Murphy Radio, and in 1960 it moved to Letchworth Garden City, where you still find it today. In 2000 Ogle sold its design arm to focus solely on model making and prototyping, and it has kept investing in machines and people every year since.

  • 1988One of the first vacuum casting machines in the UK
  • 1996Among the UK's first to adopt SLA 3D printing
  • 2018The NEO800, Ogle's largest 3D printer to date
  • 2022£2m invested in prototyping and production technologies
